## 1️⃣ A MOSAIC OF RELIGIOUS BELIEFS

### (a) The Integration of Cults — mnemonic: **DAR**
- **D — Disseminate:** Brahmanical ideas spread via **Puranic texts** composed/compiled in **simple Sanskrit verse**, deliberately made accessible to **WOMEN & SHUDRAS** (both excluded from Vedic learning).
- **A + R — Accept & Rework:** Brahmanas accepted and reworked beliefs/practices of **other social categories**.
- Result: **continuous dialogue between "great" Sanskritic Puranic traditions and "little" traditions** throughout the land.
- ⭐ **STAR EXAMPLE — PURI (Odisha):** principal deity identified by the **12th century** as **JAGANNATHA** ("**lord of the world**") = a **form of VISHNU**, depicted with **sister SUBHADRA (centre) + brother BALABHADRA**.
- **Local goddesses:** often worshipped as **a stone smeared with ochre**; incorporated into Puranic framework as **WIVES of male deities** — equated with **LAKSHMI** (Vishnu's wife) or **PARVATI** (Shiva's wife).
- **TANTRIC practices** (associated with goddess-worship):
  - **Widespread** across the subcontinent (Bengal? — no need; say "several parts")
  - **Open to WOMEN AND MEN** — unusual equality
  - Practitioners **IGNORED caste & class differences** within ritual context
  - Influenced **SHAIVISM as well as BUDDHISM** — especially **eastern, northern, southern** subcontinent
  - Evidence: sculpture of Buddhist goddess **MARICHI** (c. 10th century, Bihar) — integration exemplar

### (b) Difference and Conflict
- **Vedic vs Puranic divergence:**
  - Vedic chief deities **AGNI, INDRA, SOMA → became marginal** (rare in texts/art)
  - **Vishnu, Shiva, goddess** appear in Vedic mantras but have **little in common with Puranic mythologies**
  - **HOWEVER: the Vedas remained revered as authoritative** throughout
- **Conflict points (4):**
  1. Vedic traditionists **condemned** worship beyond **sacrifices + precisely chanted mantras**
  2. Tantric practitioners **ignored the authority of the Vedas**
  3. Devotees **projected their chosen deity (Vishnu or Shiva) as SUPREME**
  4. Tension (sometimes open conflict) with **Buddhism & Jainism**
- **Context for bhakti's rise:** devotional worship had a **~1000-year history**; forms ranged from **routine temple worship** to **ecstatic trance-like adoration**; **singing & chanting of devotional compositions** was central — especially in **VAISHNAVA & SHAIVA sects**.

### 🧠 Hooks
- **DAR** = Disseminate, Accept, Rework | Proof: **PURI-JAGANNATHA**
- Faded Vedic trio = **A-I-S** (Agni-Indra-Soma)
- Tantric keywords = "**W-I-B**" (Women open, Ignore caste, influenceBuddhism+Shaivism)

## 2️⃣ POEMS OF PRAYER: EARLY TRADITIONS OF BHAKTI

### (a) How the movement worked
- **Poet-saints emerged as LEADERS** around whom **communities of devotees** developed.
- **Brahmanas remained intermediaries** in several bhakti forms — BUT traditions **accommodated WOMEN & "LOWER CASTES"** (ineligible for liberation in orthodox Brahmanical view).
- Hallmark = **remarkable DIVERSITY**.
- **Historians' classification (2 categories):**
  - **SAGUNA** = "with attributes" → worship of specific deities: **Shiva, Vishnu + avatars, goddess/Devi forms**
  - **NIRGUNA** = "without attributes" → **abstract/formless** divine

### (b) Alvars & Nayanars of Tamil Nadu (c. 6th century onwards)
- **ALVARS** ("those **immersed** in devotion to **VISHNU**") • **NAYANARS** (devotees of **SHIVA**)
- Travelled place to place **singing TAMIL hymns** → those places became **shrines**
- **Caste backgrounds: artisan, cultivator, "untouchable" (outcaste) communities**
- **Protest angle:** some historians = **protest against Buddhism & Jainism** (dominant then, backed by merchant/artisan communities + occasional royal patronage); later the traditions **integrated into Brahmanical order**

### (c) The two great anthologies (by 10th century)
| Anthology | Composers | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **NALAYIRA DIVYAPRABANDHAM** | the **12 ALVARS** | "Four Thousand Sacred Compositions" • described as the **TAMIL VEDA** |
| **TEVARAM** | **Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar** (Nayanars) | compiled & classified **10th century on the basis of the MUSIC of the songs** |

### (d) Source extracts (memorise the punch-lines)
- **📜 TONDARADIPPODI** (a **BRAHMANA Alvar**) to Vishnu: *"You manifestly like those 'servants' who express love for your feet, **though they may be born OUTCASTES**, more than the **CHATURVEDINS** (Brahmanas versed in the four Vedas) who are strangers without allegiance to your service."*
  - → devotion > birth; note the irony: he himself is a Brahmana
- **📜 APPAR** (Nayanar): *"O **ROGUES who quote the law books**, of what use are your **GOTRA and KULA**? Just bow to **Marperu's lord (Shiva)** as your sole refuge."*
  - → open mockery of law-book ritualism

### (e) Women devotees (guaranteed 3-marker)
- **Striking feature = PRESENCE OF WOMEN** in these traditions.
- **ANDAL** (woman **ALVAR**): saw herself as the **BELOVED of VISHNU**; verses = love for the deity; **still sung today** (incl. as wedding songs).
- **KARAIKKAL AMMAIYAR** (woman **NAYANAR**): took **EXTREME ASCETICISM**; described herself as the **female PEY (demoness)** — *"bulging veins, protruding eyes, white teeth, shrunken stomach, red hair, jutting teeth, lengthy shins"* — **shouting & wailing** while wandering the forest of **ALANKATU**, home of **Shiva who DANCES, matted hair thrown in 8 directions**.
  - A **12th-century BRONZE IMAGE** of her survives.
- **Common point:** both **renounced social obligations** but **did NOT join alternative orders / become nuns**; their very existence + compositions = **CHALLENGE TO PATRIARCHAL NORMS**.

### (f) Relations with the state
- Timeline of Tamil polities: **chiefdoms (early 1st millennium) → PALLAVAS & PANDYAS (c. 6th–9th centuries CE)**
- **Buddhism & Jainism** long prevalent there (merchant/artisan support) + **occasional royal patronage**
- **Major theme of Tamil bhakti hymns = OPPOSITION to Buddhism & Jainism**, strongest in **NAYANAR** compositions; historians' explanation = **competition for royal patronage**
- **CHOLAS (9th–13th centuries) = the great patrons:** **land grants + temple construction** for Vishnu & Shiva
- **Magnificent Shiva temples built under Cholas: CHIDAMBARAM, THANJAVUR, GANGAIKONDACHOLAPURAM** → mnemonic **C-T-G**
- Era of **spectacular BRONZE sculptures of Shiva** — *"visions of the Nayanars inspired artists"*
- Both **Nayanars & Alvars were revered by VELLALA PEASANTS** → rulers courted that popularity too
- **Kings' motives:** claim **divine support + proclaim power/status** via **splendid temples adorned with stone & metal sculpture** recreating the saints' visions; saints **"sang in the language of the people"**; kings also **introduced singing of Tamil Shaiva hymns in temples** under royal patronage

### 🧠 Hooks
- **A-V / N-S** (Alvar→Vishnu, Nayanar→Shiva)
- Anthologies: "**ND = Tamil Veda (Alvars)**", "**T = ASS (Appar-Sambandar-Sundarar), sorted by MUSIC**"
- Women pair: "**Andal = Bride; Ammaiyar = Demoness (Pey)**"
- Chola temples = **C-T-G**

## 3️⃣ THE VIRASHAIVA TRADITION (12th century, Karnataka)

- **Leader: BASAVANNA** — a **BRAHMANA** and a **MINISTER** at the court; companions: **Allama Prabhu, Akkamahadevi** (women vachana composers too).
- Followers = **VIRASHAIVAS** ("heroes of Shiva") = **LINGAYATS** ("wearers of the **LINGA**").
- **Sacred texts: VACHANAS** (literally "**sayings**") — in **KANNADA**, composed orally, later collected.
- **Worship:** Shiva; devotees wore a small **linga**; revered the **JANGAMA** (wandering monk).
- **What they REJECTED (4):**
  1. **Theory of REBIRTH**
  2. **The CASTE system**
  3. **Authority of the VEDAS**
  4. **Ideas of POLLUTION / purity**
- **Distinct practices (because rebirth rejected):**
  - **BURIED their dead** (no cremation!) — logic: if no rebirth, why burn?
  - Encouraged **POST-PUBERTY MARRIAGE**
  - Encouraged **WIDOW REMARRIAGE**
  - Devotee categories **JOGATIS & JANGAMAS** exist to this day.
- **📜 Source 4 — Basavanna's vachana ("Rituals and the real world"):**
  *"When they see a **SERPENT CARVED IN STONE** they pour **MILK** on it. If a **REAL serpent** comes they say: 'Kill. Kill.' To the **SERVANT of the god who could eat** if served they say: 'Go away! Go away!' But to the **IMAGE of the god which cannot eat** they offer dishes of food."*
  - **His attitude:** mocks **empty ritualism**; ritual energy should go to **living beings/human service**.
  - **How he convinces:** **CONTRAST/paradox** (stone vs real; fed image vs hungry servant) using **everyday images** — oral, punchy vachana form.

### 🧠 Hooks
- **B-B-W** rejections: **B**edas (Vedas), **B**irth-cycle (rebirth), **W**holeness rules (purity) — plus Caste
- Practices chain: **"Vachana → Linga → Jangama → Burial → Widow remarriage"**

### 🗣️ BLURT
*"Text & language? Who was Basavanna? Why burial? Two marriage reforms? The serpent vachana's argument?"*

### 🎯 Exam angle
- **Source-based favourite** (the vachana) — questions: attitude to rituals + method of persuasion + who followed him.
- MCQ: burial (not cremation) • Kannada vachanas • jangama = wandering monk.

## 4️⃣ NORTH INDIA: WHY BHAKTI ARRIVED LATE — mnemonic **R-N-T**

- After mid-1st millennium: **Vishnu & Shiva worshipped in temples** (often ruler-built) — **BUT no evidence of Alvar/Nayanar-style devotional compositions in north India till the 14th century.**
- **R — RAJPUT STATES:** many emerged in north India; **BRAHMANAS occupied positions of importance** (secular + ritual functions); **little/no attempt to challenge them directly.**
- **N — NATHS, JOGIS, SIDDHAS** gained ground **outside** the orthodox framework:
  - Many from **ARTISANAL groups incl. WEAVERS** — rising with **organised craft production**
  - Demand boosted by **new urban centres + long-distance trade with Central & West Asia**
  - **Questioned the authority of the Vedas**
  - Spoke in **languages of ordinary people** → ancestors of today's languages
  - **LIMITATION: could NOT win support of ruling elites**
- **T — TURKS:** arrival culminating in the **DELHI SULTANATE (13th century)**:
  - **Undermined Rajput states + their Brahmanas**
  - Marked changes in culture & religion
  - **Coming of the SUFIS = significant part of these developments**

## 5️⃣ NEW STRANDS IN THE FABRIC: ISLAMIC TRADITIONS

### (a) Faiths of rulers and subjects
- **Pre-Islamic contacts:** Arab merchants on **west-coast ports** (1st millennium CE); **Central Asian settlers** in the north-west; from the **7th century** these zones joined the **Islamic world**.
- **Political milestones (memorise the 3):**
  1. **711 CE — Arab general MUHAMMAD QASIM conquered SIND** → part of the **CALIPH'S domain**
  2. **c. 13th century — TURKS & AFGHANS established the DELHI SULTANATE** (+ Deccan & other sultanates)
  3. **16th century — MUGHAL EMPIRE** (+ 18th-century regional states)
  → Islam = **acknowledged religion of rulers** in several areas.
- **The ULAMA** (pl. of *alim*, "one who knows") = scholars of Islamic studies — **religious, juridical, teaching** functions; rulers were **theoretically guided by them** to rule per the **SHARI'A**.
- **📦 Box — Shari'a sources (4):** **QUR'AN + HADIS** (traditions of the Prophet — remembered words & deeds); with expansion outside Arabia, added **QIYAS** (reasoning by analogy) + **IJMA** (consensus of the community). → chant "**Q-H-Q-I**".
- **The ZIMMI category:** from Arabic **zimma = protection**; for **people of revealed scriptures (Jews, Christians)** under Muslim rule; they paid a tax called **JIZYA** and gained the **right to protection**; **in India this status was extended to HINDUS too**.
- **Mughal self-image:** emperors of **ALL peoples, not just Muslims** (→ Chapter 9 link).
- **Rulers' flexible policy (2 proof-points):** **land endowments + tax exemptions** to **Hindu, Jaina, Zoroastrian, Christian & Jewish** institutions; respect/devotion towards non-Muslim religious leaders — made by rulers incl. **BOTH AKBAR AND AURANGZEB**.
  - **📜 Akbar's FARMAN (1598) — "A church in Khambat":** allows **padris (fathers) of the Holy Society of JESUS** to build a **church** at **Kambayat (Khambat, GUJARAT)**; city dignitaries *"should in no case stand in their way"*; *"the order of the Emperor should be obeyed in every way."*
  - **📜 Aurangzeb's LETTER (1661–62) — "Reverence for the Jogi":** to **Shiv Murat, Guru Anand Nath Jio**; sends **cloth for a cloak + 25 rupees** as offering; *"write to us whenever there is any service."*
  - → **Use this pair to destroy the myth "Akbar liberal vs Aurangzeb orthodox"** in 3-markers.

### (b) The popular practice of Islam
- **ULAMA** wrote **"confessional literature"** critiquing divergent practices — but **diverse modes of faith flourished** anyway:
- **KHOJAHS** (a branch of the **ISMAILIS — a SHIA sect**):
  - Devotional poems = **GINAN** (from Sanskrit **jnana = knowledge**)
  - Languages: **Punjabi, Multani, Sindhi, Kachchi, Hindi, Gujarati**
  - **Transmitted ORALLY** by trained **PIRS**
  - Recorded from the **17th century** in the **KHOJAKI SCRIPT** — derived from **LANDA** ("clipped" mercantile script)
  - Community spread: **Punjab, Sind, Gujarat**
- **MALABAR COAST (Kerala) Muslims:** descendants of **Arab traders**; adopted local language **MALAYALAM** + customs of **MATRILINY** (descent/inheritance through the female line) & **MATRILOCAL RESIDENCE** (wife stays in her natal home; husbands visit).
- **MOSQUE ARCHITECTURE = universal + local blend:**
  - **Universal:** orientation towards **MECCA**; **MIHRAB** (prayer niche) & **MINBAR** (pulpit) placed to indicate that direction
  - **Local variation:** **roofs & building materials** — e.g., Kerala mosque (c. 13th century) with **SHIKHARA-LIKE roof**

### (c) Names for communities (subtle 3-marker)
- Sanskrit texts & inscriptions (**8th–14th centuries**): the term **MUSALMAN/Muslim was virtually NEVER used**.
- People identified by **REGION of origin:** **TURUSHKA** (Turks) • **TAJIKA** (from Tajikistan) • **PARASHIKA** (from Persia)
- Terms like **mlechchha** signified outsiders **outside the norms of caste society** — modern religious labels hardened only gradually.

### 🧠 Hooks
- Rulers' timeline: "**711 → 13th → 16th**" (Qasim → Sultanate → Mughals)
- Shari'a = **Q-H-Q-I** | Zimmi→**Jizya**; extended to **Hindus**
- Proof pair: "**Akbar-church (1598), Aurangzeb-Jogi (1661)**"
- Khojah chain: "**Ismaili → ginan → oral pirs → Khojaki-from-landa (17th c.)**"
- Region names: **T-T-P** (Turushka-Tajika-Parashika)

## 6️⃣ THE GROWTH OF SUFISM: KHANQAHS AND SILSILAS

- **SUFIS** = Muslim **mystics**: sought **PROXIMITY TO ALLAH** through **lifelong devotion & divine love**; encouraged **ASCETICISM & SIMPLE LIVING**; **rejected the ostentation** of worldly clergy.
- **Khanqah system (from the 11th century):** communities organised around a **HOSPICE (khanqah — Persian word)** controlled by a **teaching master**:
  - Master = **SHAIKH (Arabic) / PIR or MURSHID (Persian)**
  - Enrolled **disciples = MURIDS**; appointed a **successor = KHALIFA**
  - Established **rules for spiritual conduct** + interaction between **inmates** and between **laypersons & master**
- **SILSILAS crystallised in the Islamic world around the 12th century.**
  - **SILSILA** (literally "**CHAIN**") = **continuous link between master & disciple**, stretching as an **UNBROKEN SPIRITUAL GENEALOGY to PROPHET MUHAMMAD** — through this channel flowed **spiritual power & blessings** to devotees.
- **Initiation rituals (3):** **OATH OF ALLEGIANCE** → wearing a **PATCHED GARMENT** → **SHAVED HAIR**.
- **After the shaikh's death:** tomb-shrine = **DARGAH** (Persian: "**COURT**") → became the **centre of devotion** for followers.
- **Key sufi terms:**
  - **WALI** (pl. **AULIYA**) = "friend of God" claiming **proximity to Allah**
  - **BARAKAT** = His **grace** (capable of **miracles = KARAMAT**)
- **Silsila names** came from **founder or place of origin:** **CHISHTI** (town of **Chisht**, Central Asia) • **QADIRI** (Abdul Qadir **Jilani**) • **SUHRAWARDI** (Shihabuddin Suhrawardi) • **NAQSHBANDI** (Baha'uddin **Naqshband**).
- **Outside the khanqah:** mystics **indifferent to the shari'a** ("**BE-SHARI'A**") took to **MENDICANCY & CELIBACY**, ignored rituals, were extolled as above worldly life = the **QALANDARS**.

### 🧠 Hooks
- Organisation chain: "**SPM → M-K-K**" (Shaikh/Pir/Murshid → Murid, Khalifa, Khanqah)
- Post-death quartet: "**D-W-B-K**" (Dargah, Wali, Barakat, Karamat)
- **Qalandars = "beyond-rules wanderers"**

## 7️⃣ THE CHISHTIS IN THE SUBCONTINENT ⭐⭐ (highest exam-yield section)
**Why most influential:** earliest silsila in India + **adapted successfully to the local environment** + **austere, simple lifestyle** + maintained **distance from worldly power**.

### (b) Life in the Chishti khanqah ⭐ SOURCE BOX (learn almost verbatim)
- **Shaikh NIZAMUDDIN's hospice (c. 14th century): GHIYASPUR, banks of the YAMUNA**, outskirts of **Delhi**.
- **Layout:** several **small rooms** + a **BIG HALL = JAMA'AT KHANA** (inmates & visitors **lived and prayed** there)
- **Inmates:** family members of the Shaikh + his **attendants + disciples**
- Shaikh lived in a **small room ON THE ROOF** of the hall; met visitors **MORNING & EVENING**
- **Veranda** surrounded the **courtyard**; a **boundary wall** ran around the complex
- During **MONGOL-INVASION fears**, people from neighbouring areas **flocked to the khanqah for REFUGE**
- **Open kitchen = LANGAR**, run on **FUTUH (UNASKED-FOR CHARITY)**; from morning till late night people came; **inmates cooked & served**; visitors of **all faiths** visited
- **Discussion points examiners love:** khanqah as **centre of social life**; charity + shelter + spiritual guidance in one space.

### (c) Ziyarat & qawwali — "**Z-S-Q**"
- **ZIYARAT** = pilgrimage to **tombs of sufi saints (dargahs)** — popular among **Muslims, Hindus, Sikhs...**; belief: the saint's **soul/barakat** helps **fulfil wishes**; continues today (e.g., **Ajmer — Muinuddin Chishti**).
- **📜 JAHANARA BEGUM** (princess, **daughter of Shah Jahan**) at **Nizamuddin's dargah**: her devotion gesture — **taking off her rose head-scarf and placing it on top of the blessed tomb**; presents the dargah as a **special, wish-granting, blessed place**.
- **Music & dance as part of ziyarat:** **mystical chants** by **specially trained musicians = QAWWALS** to evoke **DIVINE ECSTASY**.
- Sufis remember God by: **ZIKR** (reciting the **DIVINE NAMES**) or **SAMA'** (literally "**AUDITION**" — performance of **mystical music**).
- **SAMA' was INTEGRAL to the Chishtis** and exemplified their **interaction with INDIGENOUS devotional traditions**.
- **AMIR KHUSRAU (1253–1325)** — great poet, musician, **DISCIPLE OF NIZAMUDDIN AULIYA**:
  - Introduced the **QAUL** (Arabic: "**saying**") = hymn sung at the **OPENING or CLOSING of qawwali**
  - Followed by sufi poetry in **PERSIAN, HINDAVI or URDU** (sometimes mixed)
  - **Qawwals at Nizamuddin's shrine STILL BEGIN with the qaul**; qawwali today in **shrines all over the subcontinent**
- **📜 DARGAH QULI KHAN** (18th-century **Deccan visitor**; book = **MURAQQA-I DEHLI / "Album of Delhi"**) on **Nasiruddin Chiragh-i Dehli's shrine**:
  - *"The Shaikh (in the grave) is not the lamp of Delhi but of the **ENTIRE COUNTRY**"*
  - Crowds especially on **SUNDAY**; in the **month of DIWALI the entire population of Delhi** visits, stays in **tents** around the **spring tank** for days
  - People **bathe to obtain CURES from chronic diseases**
  - ***"Muslims and Hindus pay visits in the SAME SPIRIT"*** + **merrymaking** under the trees
  - → **The single best quote for "shared/syncretic devotional culture" answers.**
- **📜 CHARKHANAMA ("spinning-wheel song"):** spinning as devotion — **ZIKR-I JALI** (taking cotton) → **ZIKR-I QALBI** (separating) → **ZIKR-I AINI** (spooling); *"threads of breath counted one by one... up to **TWENTY-FOUR THOUSAND**... offer this to your **PIR** as a gift"* → sufism woven into **WOMEN'S HOUSEHOLD LABOUR**.

### (d) Languages and communication — "**H-P-D**"
- **H — HINDAVI:** Delhi Chishtis **conversed in HINDAVI** (the language of the people).
- **P — PADMAVAT:** long poems (**MASNAVIS**) expressed **divine love through HUMAN LOVE as allegory**; genre = **PREM-AKHYAN (love story)**; **PADMAVAT by MALIK MUHAMMAD JAYASI** = romance of **PADMINI & RATANSEN, king of CHITTOR**; their trials = **the soul's journey to the divine**; recited in **hospices, usually during SAMA'**.
  - Also: **Baba Farid's local-language verses are preserved in the GURU GRANTH SAHIB**.
- **D — DAKHANI (Bijapur, Karnataka):** **short poems in DAKHANI (variant of URDU)** by 17th–18th-century **Chishti sufis**; probably **sung by WOMEN during household chores (grinding grain, spinning)**; other forms: **LURINAMA (lullabies)** + **SHADINAMA (wedding songs)**.
  - Inspiration: pre-existing bhakti — **Kannada VACHANAS of the Lingayats + Marathi ABHANGS of the sants of PANDHARPUR**
  - **Impact: Islam gradually gained a place in the VILLAGES OF THE DECCAN** through this medium.

### (e) Sufis and the state — "**A-I-L**"
- **Chishti hallmark:** **AUSTERITY + DISTANCE from worldly power** — yet **NOT absolute isolation from politics**:

## 7️⃣ THE SACRED CENTRE — "**V&V**" ⭐

### (a) Choosing a capital (why Hampi?)

- **Other traditions:** **PAMPADEVI**, the local **mother goddess**, **did penance in these hills to MARRY VIRUPAKSHA** — the **guardian deity of the kingdom**, recognised as **a FORM OF SHIVA**.

### (c) VIRUPAKSHA TEMPLE

- **Temple halls were used for (3):**
  1. Images of gods placed to **WITNESS special programmes** — music, dance, drama
  2. **Celebrating MARRIAGES of deities** (kalyana mandapa)
  3. **SWING PAVILIONS** — deities swung on swings; **special images** (distinct from the small central-shrine idol) were used on these occasions.
