# 🟤 THEME 1: BRICKS, BEADS AND BONES — HARAPPAN CIVILISATION (5M)

**The chapter in five sentences:** The Harappan civilisation ran from **6000 BCE to 1300 BCE** in three phases — **Early Harappan 6000–2600, Mature (urban) 2600–1900, Late (decline) 1900–1300**. Its cities were planned: **Citadel (west, higher ground) + Lower Town (east, walled)** on **baked-brick platforms**, with the drainage that **Ernest Mackay** called "**certainly the most complete ancient system as yet discovered**." Its script remains **undeciphered — 375 to 400 signs, not alphabetical, written right to left, longest inscription about 26 signs**. Burials and artefact distribution reveal social differences, but no king is provable: the "**priest-king**" statue and "**palace**" labels are guesses borrowed from Mesopotamia. By **c. 1800 BCE** most Mature sites in regions like **Cholistan were abandoned**, and the decline is explained environmentally — **tectonic upheavals, river shifts, deforestation** — while **Wheeler's "Aryan massacre" theory was rejected by George Dales** (skeletal evidence disproved it).

| Recall block | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | **Bull (oxen) for ploughing**; terracotta **plough models at Cholistan and Banawali**; **ploughed field with two furrows at right angles at Kalibangan**; **canals at Shortughai (Afghanistan)**; wells elsewhere |
| Houses | **Courtyard-centred**, cooking and weaving in courtyard; **no ground-level windows** (privacy); **own bathroom, paved with bricks**; **~700 wells in Mohenjodaro** |
| Citadel | **Warehouse** + **Great Bath** (rectangular tank, **gypsum mortar watertight**, steps **north and south**, large well in side room) |
| Burials | Dead in pits, some **brick-lined**; **pottery + ornaments** (afterlife belief); **jewellery with both men and women**; **copper mirrors**; did **not** bury precious things |
| Utilitarian vs luxury | **Utilitarian = querns, pottery, needles, flesh-rubbers** (everywhere); **luxury = faience, rare materials** concentrated in **big cities** (miniature faience pots absent even at **Kalibangan**); **gold rare, from hoards** |
| Crafts | **Chanhudaro (< 7 hectares)** = craft-production town: bead-making, shell-cutting, metal-work, seal-making, weights; **carnelian red obtained by firing**; **specialised drills at Chanhudaro, Lothal, Dholavira**; **Nageshwar + Balakot = shell work** |
| Seals & weights | Seals and **sealings** for long-distance communication (intact sealing = bag untampered + sender's identity); weights of **chert, cubical, unmarked**; **lower denominations binary (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32), higher decimal (160, 200, 320, 640)** |
| Authority | Uniform bricks **1:2:4 ratio "from Jammu to Gujarat"**; debate: **no rulers / several rulers / a single state** |
| Scholars | **Daya Ram Sahni** (Harappa excavations, 1921), **R. D. Banerji** (Mohenjodaro, 1922), **John Marshall** (first Director-General of ASI), **Alexander Cunningham** (earliest, biased towards early Buddhist remains) |

**🧠 Hooks:** "**6-26-19-13**" (phases) • "**drains-privacy-700-wells**" • "**binary-decimal weights**" • "**Indra-accused-Dales-answers**" (Wheeler vs Dales) • "**Sahni-Banerji-Marshall-Cunningham**."

**🗣️ Blurt:** Three phases with dates? Why courtyard houses + privacy proof? Great Bath's watertightness? Two proofs of social difference (burials + luxuries)? Why is the script "not alphabetical"? Weights sequence? Three rival views of rulership? Two environmental decline causes?

**🎯 Traps:** Mature phase = **2600–1900** (not 1900–1300). **Shortughai = Afghanistan**, not Punjab. Faience pots absent at **Kalibangan**. **Priest-king = a guessed label**. Longest inscription = **26 signs**, signs = **375–400**.

# 🟤 THEME 2: KINGS, FARMERS AND TOWNS (13M — THE BIGGEST)

**The chapter in five sentences:** From **c. the sixth century BCE** the subcontinent changed fast — **cities, punch-marked coins, sixteen mahajanapadas and paddy transplantation** — and **Magadha rose to dominance** because of **grain, iron, elephants and easy Ganga communication (GIEE)**. The **Mauryas** followed — **Chandragupta c. 321 BCE (with Kautilya's Arthashastra and Megasthenes' Indica)**, then **Asoka (c. 272/268–231 BCE)** whose **dhamma** and inscriptions in **Prakrit using Brahmi (mostly)** we read through **James Prinsep's 1838 decipherment**; Asoka styled himself **devanampiya ("beloved of the gods") and piyadassi ("pleasant to behold")**, never naming himself. Post-Maurya, **Indo-Greeks issued the first coins with rulers' names and images** and **Kushanas the first gold coins**, calling themselves **devaputra**. Production deepened — **iron-tipped ploughshare, paddy transplantation, wells-tanks-canals (Sudarshana lake repaired by the Shaka Rudradaman, Sanskrit, no tax)** — and **land grants on copper plates** (like **Prabhavati Gupta's**) from the early centuries CE mark growing differentiation in the countryside.

| Recall block | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| Magadha's five Mauryan centres | **Pataliputra, Taxila, Ujjayini, Tosali, Suvarnagiri ("P-T-U-T-S")** |
| Asoka's machinery | **Pativedakas** (reporters, informing him "whether eating or in the inner apartments"); **dhamma mahamattas** appointed to spread dhamma; Kalinga war c. **260 BCE** |
| Coin ladder | **Punch-marked silver-copper (6th BCE) → Indo-Greeks (names + images, 2nd BCE) → Kushana gold (1st CE)**; **Yaudheyas** = republic issuing coins |
| Trade terms | Merchants = **masattuvan (Tamil), setthis/satthavahas (Prakrit)**; **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** (1st CE Greek sailor's manual; Erythraean = **Red Sea**; Malabar coast: **pepper + malabathrum**); **Kodumanal (Tamil Nadu) bead industry** |
| Cities | **Pataliputra (riverine), Ujjayini (land routes), Puhar (coast), Mathura (commercial-cultural-political)**; fine **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)**; **Xuan Zang (7th c. CE) saw Pataliputra in ruins** |
| Land grants | Early centuries CE, **most on copper plates**, to **Brahmanas/religious institutions**, mostly **Sanskrit**; **Prabhavati Gupta** (daughter of **Chandragupta II, married into Vakatakas**) = rare woman grantor; debate = **expanding agriculture** vs **weakening royal power** |
| Epigraphy | **Prinsep, an East India Company officer, deciphered Brahmi in 1838**; **Kharosthi cracked via Indo-Greek bilingual coins** (e.g., "Apollodotus"); limits: routine practices unrecorded, faint letters, undeciphered scripts, **words in brackets = epigraphists' additions** |
| Timeline spine | **327–325 Alexander • 321 Chandragupta • 272/268–231 Asoka • c. 185 BCE Mauryas end • c. 100 BCE–200 CE Shakas + Roman trade • c. 320 Guptas • 335–375 Samudragupta • 375–415 Chandragupta II • 606–647 Harshavardhana • 712 Arabs in Sind** |

**🧠 Hooks:** "**6th-BCE turning**" • "**GIEE**" • "**dhamma + 5 centres**" • "**punch-IndoGreek-Kushangold**" • "**Mackenzie collects (8,000+), Prinsep cracks, Cunningham compiles**."

**🗣️ Blurt:** Why is the 6th century BCE a turning point? Four reasons for Magadha's rise? Asoka's inscriptions — language, script, titles, what they omit? Transplantation's benefit and cost? Sudarshana lake's three phases? Prabhavati Gupta — why exceptional? The land-grant debate? How were Brahmi and Kharosthi deciphered? Limits of epigraphy?

**🎯 Traps:** **Prinsep 1838**, not Cunningham. **Arthashastra = Kautilya**. **devaputra = Kushanas**. First gold coins = **Kushanas**. Earliest inscriptions = **Prakrit**, not Sanskrit. **Rudradaman = Shaka (mlechchha)** yet wrote in **Sanskrit**. **Gandatindu Jataka** = wicked kings; subjects fled to forest — peasants' view of the state.

# 🟤 THEME 3: KINSHIP, CASTE AND CLASS (10M)

**The chapter in five sentences:** The **Mahabharata — composed over centuries and attributed to the sage Vyasa — was finally given its "critical edition" by V. S. Sukthankar's team at the BORI, Pune, from 1919 (about 47 years)**, and its core feud between **Kauravas and Pandavas of the Kuru lineage** reinforced **patriliny**: sons claim the father's resources including the throne. Brahmanical codes (**Dharmasutras and Dharmashastras, c. 500 BCE**, e.g., **Manusmriti**) regulated marriage — **kanyadana, exogamy, eight forms of marriage, gotra rules (c. 1000 BCE): a woman adopts the husband's gotra and same-gotra marriage is barred** — yet **Satavahana kings bore metronymics like Gotami-puta Siri-Satakani**, showing mothers' weight. The **four-varna ideal (Brahmana-Kshatriya-Vaishya-Shudra)** was repeatedly broken in reality: **Maurya origins were debated, Shungas/Kanvas were Brahmanas, Shaka "mlechchhas" like Rudradaman mastered Sanskrit, and Gotami-puta Siri-Satakani called himself "eka bamhana" while wedding a mlechchha's kin**. Women's rights were narrow — **stridhana was theirs, but Manusmriti denied women independent access to land** — and the **dice episode (Draupadi staked by Yudhisthira)** put "can wives be property?" on the epic's court table.

| Recall block | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | **kula (family), jnati (kinfolk), vamsha (lineage); patriliny vs matriliny; endogamy vs exogamy; polygyny vs polyandry** |
| Key source quotes | **Rigveda "fine sons" mantra (c. 1000 BCE, still in weddings)**; Rigveda's flexible family: "**I am a poet, my father is a physician, my mother grinds corn**" |
| Ekalavya | A **nishada**; Drona refused to teach "knowing the dharma"; Ekalavya shot **seven arrows into a dog's mouth**; gave **right thumb as guru-dakshina**; story defends hierarchy while celebrating devotion |
| Non-Kshatriya kings | **Mauryas: Buddhist texts = Kshatriyas, Brahmanical = low origin**; **Shungas + Kanvas = Brahmanas**; **Shakas = mlechchhas** but **Rudradaman rebuilt Sudarshana lake (earliest Sanskrit inscriptions)** |
| Guilds | **Jatis unlimited**; **shrenis = guilds**; **Mandasor stone inscription (c. 5th CE)**: silk weavers migrated **Lata (Gujarat) → Dashapura**, built a **sun temple** |
| Property | Paternal estate **equally among children after parents' death + special share for eldest**; **stridhana** = "a woman's wealth," children inherit it without husband's claim; **Manusmriti: 7 means of acquiring wealth for men, 6 for women (secondary)** |
| Epic verification | **B. B. Lal dug Hastinapura, 1951–52**; found Painted Grey Ware levels and **flood evidence** matching epic tradition |
| Sutas & kings | **Sutas** = bards and charioteers of kings; kings' genealogies boosted status |

**🧠 Hooks:** "**patriliny-Kaurava-Pandava**" • "**kanyadana-exogamy**" • "**Gotami-puta metronymic**" • "**Ekalavya-thumb**" • "**Maurya-debate-Shaka-mlechchha**" • "**Draupadi-question**" • "**stridhana-7-6**" • "**Lal-1951**."

**🗣️ Blurt:** Who prepared the critical edition, where, from when? Why does the epic reinforce patriliny? Gotra's two rules + the Satavahana contradiction? Four ways varna broke down? Mandasor weavers — three facts? The dice episode and the court's split verdict? What is stridhana? Why is Prabhavati Gupta cited in two different themes?

**🎯 Traps:** Critical edition began **1919** (not 1951) at **BORI Pune**. **Metronymic = named from mother**. Ekalavya lost his **right** thumb. **Mandasor = silk weavers** (not potters). **Polyandry = one woman, several husbands**. Same-gotra marriage = **barred** by Brahmanical law.

# 🟤 THEME 4: THINKERS, BELIEFS AND BUILDINGS (5M)

**The chapter in five sentences:** In the mid-first millennium BCE **at least 64 sects** competed — the **sramana** traditions of **Buddha (c. sixth–fourth BCE) and Mahavira (the 24th and last tirthankara)** questioned Vedic authority, and their ideas survive in the **Pali Tripitaka (Sutta, Vinaya, Abhidhamma)** and Prakrit Jain texts. The **Buddha's teaching** — four sights (old man, sick man, corpse, mendicant), the **middle path**, **anicca-dukkha-anatta, nibbana, metta and karuna** — spread through **stupas** funded by **kings (Satavahanas), guilds (even ivory-workers) and hundreds of ordinary women and men**, whose donations are inscribed on railings. A stupa is read from base up: **anda, harmika, yashti, chhatri, plus the railing and toranas; worshippers entered the EAST gateway and walked clockwise**, the sun's way. The **Buddha was not shown in human form** — **empty seat, stupa, wheel, tree** — until **Mahayana Buddhism (c. first century CE)** brought **Bodhisattvas and image worship**, while **bhakti and the ten avatars** folded local gods into **Puranic Hinduism** and **garbhagriha-and-shikhara temples** rose — from **Asoka's Ajivika cave shrines (3rd c. BCE)** to the **rock-cut Kailashnatha temple at Ellora (8th c.)**.

| Recall block | Must-know facts |
|---|---|
| Buddha's life-places | **Lumbini (birth), Bodh Gaya (enlightenment), Sarnath (first sermon, dhammachakka-pavattana), Kusinara (mahaparinibbana)** |
| Key texts/extracts | **Mahaparinibbana Sutta** (Ananda; thupa at four crossroads); **Punna's dialogue** (how to live among violent people — no anger); **Vinaya Pitaka** rules for monks and nuns; **kutagarashala** = "hut with a pointed roof" for debates |
| Stupa donations | Kings, **guilds — ivory workers financed part of one Sanchi gateway**, ordinary women and men, **bhikkhus and bhikkhunis** |
| Amaravati vs Sanchi | Amaravati looted: **1796 local raja; Mackenzie's drawings never published; 1854 Walter Elliot → "Elliot marbles" → Madras/London**; **H. H. Cole protested ("suicidal and indefensible policy"), lost Amaravati but saved Sanchi**; Sanchi preserved by the **Begums of Bhopal — Shahjehan Begum and Sultan Jehan Begum** (funded the museum and premises; John Marshall's volume dedicated to them) |
| Symbols | **Empty seat = meditation; stupa = mahaparinibbana; wheel = first sermon; tree = enlightenment**; **shalabhanjika** = woman whose touch makes trees flower (popular tradition absorbed into stupa art) |
| Jainism & Ajivikas | **23 tirthankaras before Mahavira**; five vows (**non-killing, non-stealing, non-lying, celibacy, non-possession**); **Asoka granted caves to Ajivikas (3rd c. BCE)** — earliest artificial caves |
| Temple growth | **Garbhagriha → shikhara over shrine → sculpted walls → halls, gateways, water**; **Deogarh (UP, 5th c.) Vishnu reclining on Sheshnag**; sculptor's cry at Ellora: "**Oh, how did I make it!**" |

**🧠 Hooks:** "**Sanchi-Begums, Amaravati-R-M-E (Raja-Mackenzie-Elliot)**" • "**Agni-Indra-Soma faded**" • "**64 sects + kutagarashala**" • "**23 tirthankaras**" • "**old-sick-corpse-mendicant**" • "**anda-harmika-yashti-chhatri**" • "**seat-stupa-wheel-tree**."

**🗣️ Blurt:** Why were stupas built (Mahaparinibbana Sutta)? Who donated? Stupa parts in order + which way did worshippers walk? Three Buddha symbols + their events? Why was Sanchi preserved but Amaravati destroyed — names and dates? How did Mahayana differ? What is a shalabhanjika? First caves — for whom, ordered by whom?

**🎯 Traps:** **East** gateway = entrance, **clockwise** pradakshina. **Elliot marbles = Amaravati** (not Sanchi). **Cole = in-situ preservation**. **Theravadins ≠ "Hinayana" self-name**. Tirthankaras before Mahavira = **23**. **Kailashnatha = Ellora, 8th century**.
