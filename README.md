# THEME 5: THROUGH THE EYES OF TRAVELLERS
## Perceptions of Society (c. 10th–17th Century) | 11 Marks

### 📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW
- Chapter studies **how traveller-accounts enrich knowledge of the past**, focusing on **3 travellers**: **Al-Biruni** (Uzbekistan, 11th c.), **Ibn Battuta** (Morocco, 14th c.), **François Bernier** (France, 17th c.).

---

## 1. WHY TRAVELLER ACCOUNTS MATTER (Introduction)
- People travelled as **workers, refugees of natural disasters, traders, merchants, soldiers, priests, pilgrims, adventurers**.
- Travellers met a **different world**: landscape, physical environment, customs, languages, beliefs, practices.
- Most **adapted**; a few **exceptional ones recorded** what they found **unusual or remarkable**.
- **NO surviving travel accounts by women**, though women travelled.
- Accounts **varied in subject**: court affairs / religious issues / architecture & monuments.
  - Example: **Abdur Razzaq Samarqandi** (diplomat from **Herat**) — most important description of **Vijayanagara (15th c.)**.
- Some travellers travelled **within the empire**: Mughal administrators recorded popular customs, folklore, traditions of their own land.
- **Travellers' value:** being from **different social-cultural environments**, they noticed **everyday practices** that **indigenous writers took for granted** (routine, "unworthy of recording").
- **Who they wrote for:** varied case by case.

**🎯 Exam Focus:** 3-markers ask "Why are traveller accounts valuable to historians?" → Answer: outsider perspective on routine practices + corroborate indigenous sources + aspects insiders ignored.

---

## 2. §1 AL-BIRUNI AND THE KITAB-UL-HIND

### 1.1 From Khwarizm to the Punjab
- Born **973 CE**, **Khwarizm** (present-day **Uzbekistan**) — a **centre of learning**; received the **best education** of his time.
- **Languages: Syriac, Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, Sanskrit.** Did **NOT know Greek** but knew **Plato & Greek philosophers** via **Arabic translations**.
- **1017:** Sultan **Mahmud invaded Khwarizm**, carried scholars/poets to capital **Ghazni** — Al-Biruni taken as a **HOSTAGE**; grew to like the city; **lived there till death at age 70**.
- **Interest in India (developed in Ghazni):**
  - **Sanskrit works on astronomy, mathematics, medicine translated into Arabic from the 8th century.**
  - **Punjab became part of Ghaznavid empire** → contacts with locals created **mutual trust & understanding**.
  - Spent **years with Brahmana priests & scholars** learning Sanskrit, studying **religious & philosophical texts**.
  - Itinerary unclear; **likely travelled widely in Punjab & northern India**.
- **Arabic travel literature** already accepted by his time — covered lands from **Sahara (west) to River Volga (north)**.
- Few in India read him **before 1500**; many outside India did.
- **📦 Box — "Translating texts, sharing ideas":** translated **Sanskrit works into Arabic** (incl. **Patanjali's grammar**); translated **Euclid (Greek mathematician) into Sanskrit** for Brahmana friends.
- **📜 Source 1 — Al-Biruni's objectives:** his work was *"a help to those who want to discuss religious questions with them (the Hindus), and as a repertory of information to those who want to associate with them."*

### 1.2 The Kitab-ul-Hind
- Written in **Arabic**; **simple and lucid**; **voluminous**; divided into **80 chapters**.
- **Subjects:** religion & philosophy, festivals, astronomy, alchemy, manners & customs, social life, weights & measures, iconography, laws, metrology.
- **Structure per chapter:** begins with a **QUESTION** → **description based on Sanskritic traditions** → ends with **COMPARISON with other cultures** (generally, not always).
- Scholars: this **"almost geometric structure"** (precision & predictability) owed to his **mathematical orientation**.
- **Intended readers:** peoples along the **frontiers of the subcontinent**.
- He knew translations/adaptations of **Sanskrit, Pali, Prakrit into Arabic** (fables → astronomy → medicine) but was **critical of their quality** and wanted to **improve on them**.

**📦 Box — "Hindu":** from **Old Persian word** (c. 6th–5th centuries BCE) for region **east of river Sindhu (Indus)**. Arabs: region = **"al-Hind"**, people = **"Hindi"**. Turks: people = **"Hindu"**, land = **"Hindustan"**, language = **"Hindavi"**. **None indicated religious identity** — religious connotations developed much later.
**📦 Box — "Metrology":** the science of measurement.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Biruni's identity — "**973 born, 1017 hostage, 80 chapters, Q-D-C structure**" (*"973-17-80-QDC"*). Languages: "**SAP HS**" (Syriac-Arabic-Persian-Hebrew-Sanskrit).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** "Write a note on Kitab-ul-Hind" (3M): Arabic + simple/lucid + 80 chapters + Q-D-C structure + mathematical orientation. MCQ trap: came as **hostage**, not envoy; knew Greek works **via Arabic**, not Greek.

## 3. §2 IBN BATTUTA'S RIHLA

### 2.1 An early globe-trotter
- *Rihla* written in **Arabic** — rich details of **social & cultural life in the subcontinent, 14th century**.
- Born in **Tangier (Morocco)** into a **respectable, educated family** famed for expertise in **shari'a (Islamic religious law)**; received **literary & scholastic education young**.
- Valued **experience through travel > books**; loved travelling.
- **Before India (set out 1332–33):** pilgrimages to **Mecca**; travelled **Syria, Iraq, Persia, Yemen, Oman, East-African trading ports**.
- Reached **Sind in 1333** (overland via Central Asia); lured by reputation of **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** as **generous patron of arts & letters**; reached Delhi via **Multan and Uch**.
- **Sultan appointed him QAZI (judge) of Delhi** — served **several years** → **fell out of favour → imprisoned** → misunderstanding cleared → **restored to imperial service**.
- **1342:** ordered to **China as Sultan's envoy to the Mongol ruler** → travelled via central India to **Malabar coast** → **Maldives (18 months as qazi)** → **Sri Lanka** → back to Malabar & Maldives → **Bengal and Assam** → ship to **Sumatra** → ship to Chinese port **Zaytun (Quanzhou)** → travelled China **up to Beijing** → **returned home 1347**.
- Account often **compared with Marco Polo** (Venice; visited China + India, late 13th century).
- **Meticulously recorded** new cultures, peoples, beliefs, values.
- **Travel times (his data):** Multan→Delhi **40 days**; Sind→Delhi **~50 days**; Daulatabad→Delhi **40 days**; Gwalior→Delhi **10 days**.
- **Hazards:** robbers (attacked several times; Multan→Delhi caravan attacked — **fellow travellers killed, survivors incl. Battuta severely wounded**); homesickness; illness. Preferred **caravans**.

**📜 Source 2 — "The bird leaves its nest" (Rihla excerpt):** left Tangier on a **Thursday, ALONE**, no fellow-traveller or caravan, *"swayed by an overmastering impulse"*, *"forsook my home as birds forsake their nests"* — **age 22**. Returned home **1354 — about 30 years** after setting out.
**📦 Box — "The lonely traveller":** attacked by **fever**, tied himself to the saddle with turban-cloth; at **Tunis** no one greeted him — *"felt so sad at heart... wept bitterly"*; one pilgrim consoled him.
- **🗺️ Map 1:** places visited in Afghanistan, Sind, Punjab (Andkhoy, Tirmidh, Balkh, Qunduz, Parwan, Kabul, Ghazna, Qandahar, Ajudahan, Multan, Uja, Hansi, Dehli, Sarasati, Abuhar; rivers Indus, Lahari, Sutlej).
- **🖼️ Fig 5.3:** Mughal painting — robbers attacking travellers; **Fig 5.4:** terracotta boat with armed passengers (Bengal temple, 17th–18th c.) — passengers carried arms due to robbery risk.

### 2.2 The "enjoyment of curiosities"
- Inveterate traveller: **North Africa, West Asia, Central Asia (possibly Russia), subcontinent, China** → back to Morocco.
- On return, **local ruler ISSUED INSTRUCTIONS that his stories be recorded**.
- By his time the subcontinent was in a **global network of communication: China (east) → north-west Africa & Europe (west)**; he visited shrines, learned men, rulers; officiated as qazi; enjoyed **cosmopolitan urban culture** (Arabic, Persian, Turkish speakers sharing ideas/anecdotes).
- Stories featured: **pious men, kings cruel & generous, ordinary men & women**; **UNFAMILIAR things highlighted** to impress readers of "distant yet accessible worlds".

**📜 Source 3 — "Education and entertainment" (Ibn Juzayy's introduction):** the ruler decreed Battuta dictate an account of **cities seen, interesting events, rulers met, distinguished men of learning, pious saints** — a narrative giving *"entertainment to the mind and delight to the ears and eyes"*.
**📦 Box — "In the footsteps of Ibn Battuta":** Persian travelogues 1400–1800 (and Indians writing on Central Asia/Iran/Ottoman lands): **Abdur Razzaq Samarqandi** (south India, **1440s**), **Mahmud Wali Balkhi** (travelled widely, **1620s** — became a sort of **sanyasi** for a time), **Shaikh Ali Hazin** (north India, **1740s** — **disappointed/disgusted**, expected red-carpet treatment). Most saw India as a **"land of wonders."**

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Battuta's route — "**M-M-D-M-S**" (Multan qazi → Maldives qazi → Delhi → Malabar → Maldives → Sri Lanka → Sumatra → China). Numbers: "**22-30-40-50**" (left at 22, travelled 30 yrs, Multan 40 days, Sind 50 days).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** 3-marker "Rihla as a source": Arabic + dictated to **Ibn Juzayy** by royal order + social-cultural detail + comparison with Marco Polo. 4-marker source-based: the postal extract (below).

## 4. §3 FRANÇOIS BERNIER — A DOCTOR WITH A DIFFERENCE
- **Portuguese arrived ~1500** → wrote detailed accounts of **Indian social customs & religious practices**; **Jesuit Roberto Nobili** translated Indian texts into European languages; **Duarte Barbosa** — detailed account of **trade & society in south India**.
- **After 1600:** growing numbers of **Dutch, English, French** travellers. Most famous French: jeweller **Jean-Baptiste Tavernier — visited India at least SIX times**; fascinated by **trading conditions**; compared India with **Iran & Ottoman empire**.
- **Italian doctor Manucci** — **never returned to Europe**, settled in India.
- **Bernier:** Frenchman; **doctor, political philosopher and historian**; came to the Mughal Empire **in search of opportunities**.
- **In India 12 years (1656–1668)**; associated with Mughal court: **physician to Prince Dara Shukoh** (eldest son of Shah Jahan), later **intellectual & scientist with Danishmand Khan** (an **Armenian noble**).

### 3.1 Comparing "East" and "West"
- Travelled widely; **frequently compared India with Europe**; dedicated major writing to **Louis XIV (king of France)**; other works as **letters to influential officials/ministers**.
- Described India as a **"bleak situation"** vs Europe — assessment **not always accurate** — yet writings **extremely popular**.
- **📜 Source 4 — "Travelling with the Mughal army"** (march to Kashmir): kept **two Turkoman horses**, Persian camel & driver, groom, cook, servant with water-flask; tent, carpet, portable cane bed, pillow, mattress, leather table-cloths, napkins, culinary bags; **excellent rice for 5–6 days, sweet biscuits with anise, limes, sugar; linen bag with iron hook to drain dahi (curds)** — *"nothing so refreshing as lemonade and dahi."*
- **Publication record:** published in **France 1670–71**; translated into **English, Dutch, German, Italian within 5 years**; **reprinted 8× in French (1670–1725)**; **3× in English by 1684**. Contrast: Arabic/Persian accounts circulated as **manuscripts**, generally **not published before 1800**.
- **📦 Box — "Creation & circulation of ideas about India":** European travellers' printed books created an **image of India for Europeans**; after **1750**, Indians **Shaikh Itisamuddin & Mirza Abu Talib** visited Europe, **confronted this image**, tried to influence it with their own versions.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Bernier chain — "**D-D-L**" (Doctor → Dara Shukoh → Danishmand Khan); publication — "**1670-71, 4 languages, 8 French, 3 English**".
**🎯 Exam Focus:** MCQ: Tavernier = **jeweller, 6 visits**; Manucci = **never returned**; Bernier served **Dara Shukoh then Danishmand Khan** (NOT Aurangzeb). 3-marker: why were Bernier's works so popular? → printing + letters to officials + Louis XIV dedication + Europe's hunger for India comparisons.

## 5. §4 MAKING SENSE OF AN ALIEN WORLD: AL-BIRUNI AND THE SANSKRITIC TRADITION

### 4.1 Overcoming barriers to understanding
- Each traveller used **distinct strategies**; travellers compared India with familiar practices.
- Al-Biruni discussed **3 BARRIERS** to understanding:
  1. **LANGUAGE** — Sanskrit so different from Arabic/Persian that ideas could not be easily translated.
  2. **DIFFERENT RELIGIOUS BELIEFS & PRACTICES.**
  3. **SELF-ABSORPTION & INSULARITY of the local population.**
- Despite awareness, he depended **almost exclusively on works of Brahmanas**, citing **Vedas, Puranas, Bhagavad Gita, works of Patanjali, Manusmriti**.
- **📦 Box — "A language with an enormous range":** Sanskrit — *"of an enormous range, both in words and inflections, something like the Arabic, calling one and the same thing by various names... using one and the same word for a variety of subjects."*
- **📦 Box — "God knows best!":** travellers did NOT believe everything; on a wooden idol supposedly **216,432 years old**, Al-Biruni asks: *"How could wood have lasted such a length of time... where the air and the soil are rather wet? God knows best!"*

### 4.2 Al-Biruni's description of the caste system
- Sought **parallels in other societies**: **ancient Persia's 4 social categories** — (i) knights & princes; (ii) monks, fire-priests, lawyers; (iii) physicians, astronomers, scientists; (iv) peasants & artisans → **social divisions NOT unique to India**.
- Also noted: **within Islam all men are EQUAL**, differing only in **piety**.
- **Disapproved of the notion of POLLUTION:** everything impure **strives to regain purity** — the **sun cleanses the air**; **salt prevents sea pollution** — otherwise life on earth impossible → social pollution (intrinsic to caste) was **contrary to the laws of nature**.
- **📜 Source 5 — "The system of varnas" (verbatim account):** **Brahmana** — created from the **HEAD of Brahman** (Brahman = "force called nature"; head = highest part) → *"choice part of the whole genus"*, *"very best of mankind"*. **Kshatriya** — **shoulders and hands**; degree not much below Brahmana. **Vaishya** — **thigh**. **Shudra** — **feet**. *"Between the latter two classes there is no very great distance."* Though classes differ, **"they live together in the same towns and villages, mixed together in the same houses and lodgings."**
- His description was shaped by **normative Sanskrit texts** (rules from the **Brahmanas' point of view**); **real life was less rigid**: **antyaja** (literally "born outside the system") provided **inexpensive labour to peasants AND zamindars** → **socially oppressed but INCLUDED in economic networks**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Barriers — "**LRS**" (Language, Religion, Self-absorption). Body map top→bottom — "**Head-Shoulders-Thigh-Feet = B-K-V-S**". His books: "**V-P-G-P-M**" (Vedas, Puranas, Gita, Patanjali, Manusmriti).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** The single most-asked 8-marker of this chapter: "Al-Biruni's understanding of caste + why it differed from reality." Must include: textual dependence + disapproval of pollution + laws of nature analogy + antyaja nuance. Source-based Qs test the varna extract.

## 6. §5 IBN BATTUTA AND THE EXCITEMENT OF THE UNFAMILIAR

### 5.1 The coconut and the paan
- Best examples of his **strategies of representation**: describing **unfamiliar plant produce** (coconut, paan) **through familiar comparisons**.
- **📜 Source 6 — The paan:** betel = tree **cultivated like the grape-vine**; **has no fruit, grown only for its LEAVES**; chewed with **areca nut** (like a nutmeg, broken into small pellets) + **a little chalk** on the leaves.
- **📜 Source 7 — "Nuts like a man's head" (coconut):** trees look **exactly like date-palms** except the fruit; **nut resembles a MAN'S HEAD** — two eyes & a mouth, green inside looks like **the brain**, attached fibre looks like **hair**; fibre made into **cords to sew up ships instead of iron nails** + **cables for vessels**.

### 5.2 Ibn Battuta and Indian cities
- Cities = **full of exciting opportunities** for those with **drive, resources, skills**; **densely populated & prosperous**; occasional disruptions from **wars & invasions**; **crowded streets, bright colourful markets** stacked with goods.
- **Delhi = "vast city, great population, the largest in India"**; **Daulatabad (Maharashtra) rivalled Delhi in size**.
- **📜 Source 8 — "Dehli":** rampart **without parallel** — wall breadth **11 cubits**; houses for **night sentry & gate-keepers** inside; **store-houses** (edibles, magazines, ammunition, ballistas, siege machines); stored grain **lasts long without rotting**; horsemen & infantrymen patrol **inside the rampart**; **windows opening on the city side give light**; **lower part STONE, upper part BRICKS**; many close-set towers; **28 gates (darwaza)** — **Budaun darwaza greatest**; **Mandwi darwaza → grain market**; **Gul darwaza → adjacent orchard**; fine **cemetery** — graves with **domes or arches**; flowers sown: **tuberose, jasmine, wild rose** — blossoms **all seasons**.

### 5.3 A unique system of communication
- **Postal system** amazed him: merchants could **send information, remit credit across long distances, dispatch goods at short notice**.
- **Efficiency proof:** Sind→Delhi normally **50 days**, but **spy news reached the Sultan in 5 days**.
- **📜 Source 9 — "On horse and on foot":** two kinds — **Horse-post = ULUQ** (royal horses stationed **every 4 miles**); **Foot-post = DAWA** (one-third of a mile; **3 stations per mile**); at every dawa a **well-populated village** with **3 pavilions** — men with **girded loins**, carrying a **rod two cubits long with COPPER BELLS**; courier runs letter+rod; relay continues to destination; **foot-post QUICKER than horse-post**; used to transport **"fruits of Khurasan"** (much desired in India).

**📜 Source 10 — "A strange nation?" (Abdur Razzaq, 1440s):** disliked **Calicut (Kozhikode, Kerala)** people — *"a people the likes of whom I had never imagined"*, a **"strange nation"**; but ~**3 leagues (9 miles) from Mangalore** saw an **idol-house** (*"the likes of which is not to be found in all the world"*): square, **~10 yards a side, 5 yards high, covered with CAST BRONZE, four porticos**; entrance portico: **full-stature GOLD statue with TWO RED RUBIES for eyes** — *"What craft and artisanship!"*

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Postal pair — "**U4-D3**" (Uluq 4 miles/horse; Dawa ⅓-mile/foot-bells) — dawa **faster**. Battuta's adjectives — cities "**D-C-C**" (Dense, Colourful, Crowded).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** Postal extract = repeat Section-D favourite. Traps: dawa is FOOT (faster); Delhi's wall = 11 cubits, 28 gates, Budaun greatest; Razzaq = Calicut disliked but Mangalore temple admired (mixed perception Q).

## 7. §6 BERNIER AND THE "DEGENERATE" EAST
- Bernier = **different intellectual tradition** from Ibn Battuta: **compared/contrasted India with Europe (esp. France)**; focused on **depressing situations**; aimed to **influence policy-makers & intelligentsia** to make the "right" decisions.
- *Travels in the Mughal Empire*: **detailed observations, critical insights, reflection**; tried to place Mughal history in a **universal framework**; **constant comparison** with contemporary Europe, **emphasising Europe's superiority**.
- **Method: BINARY OPPOSITION** — India as the **inverse of Europe**; differences **ordered hierarchically** → India = **inferior** to the Western world.

### 6.1 The question of landownership
- **Fundamental difference (per Bernier): lack of PRIVATE PROPERTY IN LAND** in Mughal India.
- He believed **private property = virtue**; **crown ownership harmful** to state & people; **emperor owned ALL land, distributed among nobles** → **disastrous consequences**. (Perception common to **most 16th–17th century travellers**, not unique to him.)
- **Chain of consequences:** crown ownership → holders **could not pass land to children** → **aversion to long-term investment** in production → **no class of "improving" landlords** (as in Western Europe) → **uniform ruination of agriculture, excessive oppression of peasantry, continuous decline in living standards** of all sections **except the ruling aristocracy**.
- **📜 Source 11 — "The poor peasant":** much land *"little more than sand, or barren mountains, badly cultivated, thinly populated"*; good land **untilled for want of labourers** (many perish from **bad treatment by Governors**); poor peasants unable to pay *"rapacious lords"* lose **means of subsistence AND their children (carried away as slaves)**; peasantry **"driven to despair... abandon the country."**
- **His purpose:** participate in **European debates on state & society**; his description = **warning** to those not recognising the "merits" of private property.
- **"No middle state":** India = **undifferentiated masses of impoverished people** subjugated by a **small, very rich & powerful ruling minority** — *no social group between poorest and richest* — Bernier: **"There is no middle state in India."**
- **His dark portrait:** king = *"king of beggars and barbarians"*; cities/towns ruined, contaminated with **"ill air"**; fields *"overspread with bushes"*, full of **"pestilential marishes" (marshes)** — all blamed on **crown ownership of land**.
- **📜 Source 12 — "A warning for Europe":** if European kings followed the Mughal model, their kingdoms would cease to be *"well-cultivated and peopled, so well built, so rich, so polite and flourishing"* — they'd be **"kings of deserts and solitudes, of beggars and barbarians."**
- **Reality-check (NCERT correction):** **NO Mughal official document** claims state = sole landowner. **Abu'l Fazl** (Akbar's chronicler) called land revenue **"remunerations of sovereignty"** — a **claim on subjects for PROTECTION, not rent on owned land**. Europeans mistook it for rent because **revenue demands were very high** — but it was **a tax on the CROP**, not land rent/land tax (→ Chapter 8).
- **Influence on Western theory:** **Montesquieu (18th c.)** developed **ORIENTAL DESPOTISM** — Asian rulers enjoy **absolute authority**; all land belongs to king; **private property non-existent**; everyone except emperor & nobles barely survives.
- **Karl Marx (19th c.):** further developed as **ASIATIC MODE OF PRODUCTION** — pre-colonial India: **surplus appropriated by the STATE** → society of **many autonomous, internally egalitarian VILLAGE COMMUNITIES**; imperial court presided, **respecting autonomy** as long as surplus flowed → regarded as a **STAGNANT system**.
- **Actual social reality:** 16th–17th c. rural society had **considerable social & economic differentiation**: big **zamindars** (superior rights) ←→ **"untouchable" landless labourers**; in between: **big peasant** (hired labour, commodity production) and **smaller peasant** (barely subsistence).

### 6.2 A more complex social reality
- Bernier's **anti-tyranny agenda** is obvious, but he **hints at complexity**:
  - Artisans had **no incentive to improve manufactures** (profits appropriated by state) → manufactures "in decline". **BUT** he conceded **vast quantities of the world's precious metals flowed INTO India** (manufactures exported for **gold & silver**) and there was a **prosperous merchant community in long-distance exchange**.
- **📜 Source 13 — "A different socio-economic scenario":** a large portion of the country **extremely fertile** — **Bengal surpasses Egypt** in **rice, corn, necessaries** + **silks, cotton, indigo** (not cultivated in Egypt); artisans compelled by necessity to produce **carpets, brocades, embroideries, gold & silver cloths, silks & cottons** for home & export; **"gold and silver, after circulating in every other quarter of the globe, come at length to be swallowed up... in Hindustan."**
- **Urbanisation:** 17th century — **~15% of population lived in towns** — **HIGHER than Western Europe's average** in the same period.
- **"Camp towns" critique:** Bernier called Mughal cities **"camp towns"** — owing their existence to the **imperial camp**; arising when the court moved in, **rapidly declining when it moved out**; **no viable social-economic foundations**, dependent on **imperial patronage**. (Historians show this was exaggerated.)
- **🖼️ Fig 5.12:** gold spoon with emeralds & rubies — dexterity of **Mughal artisans**; Fig 5.11 — 19th-c. drawing reinforcing the notion of **unchanging rural society**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Bernier's doom chain — "**EMPIRE**": **E**mperor owns → **M**otivation lost → **P**roperty not hereditary → **I**nvestment nil → **R**uination → **E**urope superior. Theory ladder — "**M-O-M**": **M**ontesquieu (Oriental despotism) → **M**arx (Asiatic mode). Counter-chain — "**B-15-C**": Bengal fertile, 15% urban, camp-town critique.
**🎯 Exam Focus:** Guaranteed 8-marker: "Bernier's comparison + why inaccurate." Include binary opposition, landownership chain, oriental despotism→Marx lineage, then corrections (Abu'l Fazl's "remunerations of sovereignty", crop-tax, 15% urban, Source 13 fertility, merchant prosperity). Source-based: "poor peasant" extract or "no middle state".

# THEME 6: BHAKTI-SUFI TRADITIONS
## Changes in Religious Beliefs and Devotional Texts (c. 8th–18th Century) | 10 Marks

### 📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW
- Traces the **evolution of religious beliefs & devotional texts** (c. 8th–18th c.): integration of cults → Alvar-Nayanar bhakti → Virashaivas → Islamic traditions → Sufism (esp. **Chishtis**) → Kabir, **Guru Nanak**, **Mirabai**.

## 1. §1 A MOSAIC OF RELIGIOUS BELIEFS

### 1.1 The integration of cults
- **Two processes** worked simultaneously:
  1. **Dissemination of Brahmanical ideas:** Puranic texts composed/compiled/preserved in **simple Sanskrit verse**, accessible to **WOMEN & SHUDRAS** (excluded from Vedic learning).
  2. **Brahmanas accepted & reworked** beliefs/practices of **other social categories**.
- Result: continuous dialogue between **"great" Sanskritic Puranic traditions** and **"little" traditions** throughout the land.
- **Prime example — PURI (Odisha):** principal deity identified by the **12th century** as **JAGANNATHA** ("lord of the world"), a **form of VISHNU** — depicted with **sister Subhadra & brother Balabhadra**.
- **Local goddesses** (often just a **stone smeared with ochre**) were incorporated into the Puranic framework as **WIVES of principal male deities** — equated with **Lakshmi** (Vishnu's wife) or **Parvati** (Shiva's wife).
- **Tantric practices** (associated with goddess-worship): **widespread** across the subcontinent; **open to women AND men**; practitioners often **ignored caste & class differences** in ritual context; influenced **Shaivism AND Buddhism** (esp. eastern, northern, southern India). (Fig: Buddhist goddess **Marichi**, c. 10th c. Bihar — integration exemplar.)

### 1.2 Difference and conflict
- **Vedic vs Puranic divergence:** Vedic deities **Agni, Indra, Soma** became **marginal**; Vishnu, Shiva & the goddess appear in Vedic mantras but with **little in common** with elaborate **Puranic mythologies** — yet **the Vedas remained revered as authoritative**.
- **Conflicts:** (i) Vedic traditionists **condemned** practices beyond **sacrifices/precisely-chanted mantras**; (ii) Tantrics **ignored Vedic authority**; (iii) devotees projected **their chosen deity (Vishnu or Shiva) as supreme**; (iv) tense relations with **Buddhism & Jainism** (sometimes open conflict).
- **Context of bhakti:** devotional worship had **~1,000 years of history**; expressions ranged from **routine temple worship** to **ecstatic, trance-like adoration**; **singing & chanting** of devotional compositions central — especially in **Vaishnava & Shaiva sects**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Integration — "**DAR**": **D**isseminate Brahmanical ideas, **A**ccept & **R**ework local ones (proof: Puri-Jagannatha; goddess→Lakshmi/Parvati). Vedic fading trio — "**AIS**" (Agni, Indra, Soma).

## 2. §2 POEMS OF PRAYER: EARLY TRADITIONS OF BHAKTI
- Evolution produced **poet-saints as leaders** with **communities of devotees**.
- Brahmanas remained **important intermediaries** in several bhakti forms — BUT traditions **accommodated women & "lower castes"** (ineligible for liberation in orthodox Brahmanical framework).
- Characterised by **remarkable diversity**.
- **Classification by historians of religion — 2 broad categories:**
  - **SAGUNA** (with attributes): worship of specific deities — **Shiva, Vishnu & his avatars, forms of the goddess/Devi**.
  - **NIRGUNA** (without attributes): worship of an abstract/formless divine.

### 2.1 The Alvars and Nayanars of Tamil Nadu
- From **c. 6th century**: **ALVARS** = "*those immersed in devotion to Vishnu*"; **NAYANARS** = devotees of **SHIVA**.
- Travelled place to place **singing Tamil hymns**; these places became **shrines**.
- Came from **diverse castes** — **artisan, cultivator, "untouchable" (outcaste) communities**.
- Some historians: their movement = **protest against Buddhism & Jainism** (dominant in the region, patronised by merchants/artisans + occasional royal support); positions later **challenged by Brahmanas**; ultimately traditions **integrated with the Brahmanical order**.
- **Anthologies (by 10th century):**
  - **NALAYIRA DIVYAPRABANDHAM** — compilation of the **12 Alvars'** compositions; **"Four Thousand Sacred Compositions"**; described as the **TAMIL VEDA**.
  - **TEVARAM** — collection of **Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar** (Nayanars); compiled & classified in the **10th century on the basis of the MUSIC of the songs**.
- **📜 Source 1 — Tondaradippodi (a BRAHMANA Alvar) on Vishnu:** *"You manifestly like those 'servants' who express their love for your feet, THOUGH THEY MAY BE BORN OUTCASTES, more than the Chaturvedins (Brahmanas versed in the four Vedas) who are strangers and without allegiance to your service."*
- **📜 Source 2 — Appar (Nayanar):** *"O rogues who quote the law books, of what use are your gotra and kula? Just bow to Marperu's lord (Shiva) as your sole refuge."*

### 2.2 Attitudes towards caste
- The **sanctity of Tamil bhakti hymns** was compared to the Vedas; saints claimed their devotion **transcended caste** — devotion (not birth) became the route to salvation; sources show both **critique of Brahmanical pretension** (Appar) and **devotees' merit over birth** (Tondaradippodi).

### 2.3 Women devotees
- **Striking feature: PRESENCE OF WOMEN.**
- **ANDAL** (woman **Alvar**): saw herself as the **BELOVED OF VISHNU**; verses express **love for the deity**; widely sung **even today**.
- **KARAIKKAL AMMAIYAR** (woman **Nayanar**, devotee of Shiva): adopted **EXTREME ASCETICISM**; compositions preserved within the Nayanar tradition; **12th-century bronze image** survives (Fig 6.4).
  - **📜 Source 3 — self-description as "female Pey (demoness)":** *"bulging veins, protruding eyes, white teeth, shrunken stomach, red hair, jutting teeth, lengthy shins"* — **wandering, shouting & wailing** in the forest of **Alankatu** — home of **Shiva who dances, matted hair thrown in eight directions**.
- Both women **renounced social obligations** but **did NOT join alternative orders or become nuns**; their existence & compositions **CHALLENGED PATRIARCHAL NORMS**.

### 2.4 Relations with the state
- Tamil region: chiefdoms (early 1st millennium) → **states: PALLAVAS & PANDYAS (c. 6th–9th centuries CE)**.
- **Buddhism & Jainism** had long prevalence there (merchant/artisan support) + **occasional royal patronage**.
- **Major theme in Tamil bhakti hymns: OPPOSITION to Buddhism & Jainism** — especially in **NAYANAR** compositions; historians explain the hostility as **competition for royal patronage**.
- **CHOLAS (9th–13th centuries)** supported **Brahmanical & bhakti traditions**: **land grants + temple construction** for Vishnu & Shiva.
- Magnificent **Shiva temples** built under Chola patronage: **CHIDAMBARAM, THANJAVUR, GANGAIKONDACHOLAPURAM**; era of spectacular **bronze sculptures of Shiva** — *"the visions of the Nayanars inspired artists."*
- Both **Nayanars & Alvars revered by VELLALA PEASANTS**; rulers sought their support too — **Chola kings claimed divine support & proclaimed power** by building **splendid temples adorned with stone & metal sculpture** recreating the saints' visions, saints who **"sang in the language of the people."** Kings also **introduced the singing of Tamil Shaiva hymns** in temples under royal patronage.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Alvar-Nayanar — "**A-Vishnu, N-Shiva**". Anthologies — "**ND = Tamil Veda, T = Appar-Sambandar-Sundarar (music-based, 10th c.)**". Women pair — "**Andal = Alvar-bride; Ammaiyar = Nayanar-demoness**". Chola temples — "**CTG**" (Chidambaram, Thanjavur, Gangaikondacholapuram).

## 3. §3 THE VIRASHAIVA TRADITION IN KARNATAKA
- A **new movement, 12th century**, led by **BASAVANNA** (a Brahmana **minister** of a Jain? king — NCERT: minister at the court) with **Allama Prabhu, Akkamahadevi** (notable women vachana composers).
- Followers = **VIRASHAIVAS (Heroes of Shiva) / LINGAYATS** (wearers of the **linga**).
- Sacred texts: **VACHANAS** (literally "sayings") in **KANNADA** — composed orally, collected later.
- Worshipped **Shiva in the form of a LINGA** worn on the body; **JANGAMA** (wandering monk) revered.
- **They QUESTIONED:** (i) the **theory of REBIRTH**, (ii) the **caste system**, (iii) Vedic authority, (iv) ideas of **pollution/purity**.
- **Distinct practices:** **FUNERALS = BURIAL** (instead of cremation — logically following rejection of rebirth); encouraged **post-puberty marriage & widow REMARRIAGE**; strength of the tradition visible in **jogatis and jangamas** (devotee categories) surviving to this day.
- **📜 Source 4 — "Rituals and the real world" (Basavanna's vachana):** *"When they see a SERPENT CARVED IN STONE they pour MILK on it. If a REAL serpent comes they say: 'Kill. Kill.' To the SERVANT of the god who could eat if served they say: 'Go away! Go away!' But to the IMAGE of the god which cannot eat they offer dishes of food."* → mocks empty ritualism; promotes **human service over symbolic ritual**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Virashaiva = "**V-B-R**" rejects (Vedas, Birth/rebirth, Ritual purity) + "**B-B-W**": Burial, Bride-price-era marriage reforms (post-puberty & widow remarriage), Worn linga.
**🎯 Exam Focus:** Source-based favourite (Basavanna vachana — "Describe his attitude towards rituals; how does he convince the listener?" → contrast/paradox of stone vs real serpent, image vs hungry devotee). 3-marker: Virashaiva practices (burial, widow remarriage, vachanas, jangama).

## 4. §4 NORTH INDIA: RELIGIOUS LEADERS OUTSIDE THE ORTHODOX FRAME
- After mid-1st millennium: **Vishnu & Shiva worshipped in temples** (often ruler-supported), **but NO evidence of Alvar-Nayanar-type devotional compositions in north India till the 14th century.**
- Why: north India saw **emergence of RAJPUT states** — **Brahmanas held important positions** (secular + ritual functions) — **little/no attempt to challenge them directly**.
- **Simultaneously, other religious leaders gained ground: the NATHS, JOGIS, SIDDHAS:**
  - Many from **ARTISANAL groups incl. WEAVERS** (growing with **organised craft production**, **new urban centres**, **long-distance trade with Central & West Asia**).
  - **Questioned the authority of the Vedas**; expressed themselves in **languages of ordinary people** (ancestors of today's languages).
  - **BUT could not win support of ruling elites.**
- **New element: coming of the TURKS → Delhi Sultanate (13th century)** — undermined Rajput states & their Brahmanas; marked changes in culture & religion; **arrival of the SUFIS** = significant part of these developments.

**🎯 Exam Focus:** 3-marker: "Why did bhakti arrive late in north India?" → Rajput-Brahmana dominance + no direct challenge; Naths/Jogis/Siddhas as popular but elite-excluded currents.

## 5. §5 NEW STRANDS IN THE FABRIC: ISLAMIC TRADITIONS
- Contacts beyond seas/mountains were millennia-old: **Arab merchants on west-coast ports** (1st millennium CE), **Central Asian settlers in the north-west**; from the **7th century (advent of Islam)** these regions joined the **Islamic world**.

### 5.1 Faiths of rulers and subjects
- **711:** Arab general **Muhammad Qasim conquered SIND** → part of the **Caliph's domain**.
- **c. 13th century:** **Turks & Afghans established the Delhi Sultanate** → later **Deccan & other Sultanates**; **Mughal Empire (16th c.)**; regional states (18th c.) — **Islam = acknowledged religion of rulers** in several areas.
- **Rulers theoretically guided by the ULAMA** (scholars of Islamic studies — religious, juridical, teaching functions) to rule per the **SHARI'A**.
- **Complication:** large non-Muslim populations → category of **ZIMMI** ("protected", from Arabic **zimma**) — for **people of revealed scriptures (Jews, Christians)** living under Muslim rulership; they paid **JIZYA** (tax) and gained **right to protection**; **in India extended to HINDUS too**.
- Mughal rulers saw themselves as **emperors of ALL peoples, not just Muslims** (→ Ch 9).
- **Rulers' flexible policy:** **land endowments & tax exemptions** to **Hindu, Jaina, Zoroastrian, Christian, Jewish institutions**; respect for non-Muslim religious leaders — by **Akbar AND Aurangzeb** both.
- **📜 Source 5 — "A church in Khambat" (Akbar's FARMAN, 1598):** orders Kambayat (Khambat, Gujarat) dignitaries to let **padris (fathers) of the Holy Society of Jesus build a church** — *"the order of the Emperor should be obeyed in every way."*
- **📜 Source 6 — "Reverence for the Jogi" (Aurangzeb's letter, 1661–62)** to **Shiv Murat, Guru Anand Nath Jio**: sends **cloak-cloth + 25 rupees** as offering; *"your Reverence may write to us whenever there is any service."*
- **📦 Box — Shari'a:** law governing the Muslim community, based on **Qur'an + hadis** (traditions of the Prophet — remembered words & deeds); with expansion outside Arabia, **QIYAS (reasoning by analogy)** and **IJMA (consensus of the community)** recognised as additional sources → shari'a evolved from **Qur'an, hadis, qiyas, ijma**.

### 5.2 The popular practice of Islam
- **Ulama** wrote **confessional literature** critiquing divergent popular practices — yet **diverse modes of faith flourished**.
- **KHOJAHS** (branch of the **Ismailis — a Shia sect**): developed **GINAN** (from Sanskrit **jnana** = knowledge) — **devotional poems in Punjabi, Multani, Sindhi, Kachchi, Hindi, Gujarati**; **transmitted ORALLY** by trained **PIRS**; recorded from the **17th century** in the **KHOJAKI SCRIPT** (derived from **landa**, "clipped" mercantile script) used by Khojahs of **Punjab, Sind, Gujarat**. (Fig 6.8: Khojaki manuscript.)
- **Arab Muslim traders on the MALABAR COAST (Kerala):** adopted the local language **MALAYALAM** + local customs of **MATRILINY & MATRILOCAL RESIDENCE** (women stay in natal home after marriage; husbands visit).
- **Mosque architecture = blend of universal + local:** universal = **orientation towards MECCA** (placement of **MIHRAB** = prayer niche; **MINBAR** = pulpit); local variation = **roofs & building materials** (e.g., Kerala mosque, c. 13th century, with **shikhara-like roof**).

### 5.3 Names for communities
- **"Hindu" & "Muslim" as community labels were NOT in currency for long.**
- Sanskrit texts & inscriptions (8th–14th c.): the term **MUSALMAN was virtually never used**; people identified by **region of origin**: **TURUSHKA** (Turks), **TAJIKA** (Tajikistan), **PARASHIKA** (Persia).
- Terms used for outsiders (like **mlechchha**) signalled that newcomers were **outside the norms of caste society** — religious identities as we know them developed gradually.

## 6. §6 THE GROWTH OF SUFISM: KHANQAHS AND SILSILAS
- **SUFIS** = Muslim **mystics**: sought **proximity to Allah** through **lifelong devotion & divine love**; encouraged **asceticism & simple living**; **rejected ostentation** of the clergy.
- **Khanqah organisation (from the 11th century):** communities around a **hospice (khanqah — Persian)** controlled by a **teaching master: SHAIKH (Arabic) / PIR or MURSHID (Persian)**; he enrolled **disciples (MURIDS)**, appointed a **successor (KHALIFA)**; established **rules for spiritual conduct** & interaction (inmates & laypersons).
- **SILSILAS crystallised in the Islamic world c. 12th century.** Silsila (lit. **"chain"**) = **continuous link between master & disciple, an unbroken spiritual genealogy to PROPHET MUHAMMAD** — channel transmitting **spiritual power & blessings**.
- **Initiation rituals:** **OATH OF ALLEGIANCE**, wearing a **PATCHED GARMENT**, **SHAVED HAIR**.
- **After the shaikh's death:** his **tomb-shrine = DARGAH** (Persian: "court") — became the **centre of devotion**.
- **Terms:** **WALI** (pl. **AULIYA**) = friend of God claiming proximity to Allah; **BARAKAT** = His grace; **KARAMAT** = miracles.
- **Silsila names** derived from founder/place: **Chishti** (Central Asian town **Chisht**), **Qadari** (Abdul Qadir Jilani), **Suhrawardi** (Shihabuddin Suhrawardi), **Naqshbandi** (Baha'uddin Naqshband).
- **6.2 Outside the khanqah:** mystics **indifferent to the shari'a** — **be-shari'a** — took to **mendicancy & celibacy**, ignored rituals, extolled as above worldly life: the **QALANDARS** (madmen/wanderers).

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Chain of terms — "**SPM-MKK**": Shaikh/Pir/Murshid → Murid, Khalifa, Khanqah. Post-death — "**D-W-B-K**": Dargah, Wali, Barakat, Karamat. Silsila names from "**F-P-F-N**" (Founder/place: Chisht, Jilani, Suhrawardi, Naqshband).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** Definitions MCQs: silsila = chain to Prophet; dargah = Persian "court"; qalandars = outside khanqah, anti-shari'a mendicants. 3-marker: silsila organisation.

## 7. §7 THE CHISHTIS IN THE SUBCONTINENT
- **Earliest silsila in India; MOST INFLUENTIAL** — because it **adapted successfully to the local environment** + **austere, simple lifestyle** + distance from worldly power.

### 7.1 Life in the Chishti khanqah
- **📜 Source 7 — Shaikh Nizamuddin's hospice (c. 14th century):** at **GHIYASPUR, on the banks of the YAMUNA**, outskirts of **Delhi**; comprised **several small rooms + a BIG HALL (JAMA'AT KHANA)** where **inmates & visitors lived and prayed**; inmates = **family members of the Shaikh, attendants, disciples**; Shaikh lived in a **small room ON THE ROOF**, meeting visitors **morning & evening**; **veranda** around the courtyard; **boundary wall**; during **Mongol-invasion fears, people flocked to the khanqah for REFUGE**; **open kitchen (LANGAR)** run on **FUTUH (unasked-for charity)**; visitors from **all faiths**; inmates **cooked & served food**; thoughts/discussions recorded.

### 7.2 Chishti devotionalism: ziyarat and qawwali
- **ZIYARAT** = pilgrimage to **tombs (dargahs)** of sufi saints — popular **across communities** (Muslims, Hindus, Sikhs...); belief: the **saint's soul (barakat) helps fulfil wishes**; practice continues (e.g., **Ajmer shrine of Muinuddin Chishti**).
- **📜 Source 7 (Jahanara Begum, princess, daughter of Shah Jahan)** at the **Nizamuddin dargah**: describes her gestures of devotion — **taking off her rose head-scarf and placing it on top of the blessed tomb** — dargah as a **special, wish-granting place**.
- **Music & dance part of ziyarat:** **mystical chants by specially trained musicians (QAWWALS)** to evoke **divine ecstasy**; sufis remember God via **ZIKR (recitation of the Divine Names)** or **SAMA'** (literally **"audition"** — mystical music performance); sama' was **integral to the Chishtis** & exemplified **interaction with indigenous devotional traditions**.
- **AMIR KHUSRAU (1253–1325)** — great poet, musician, **disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya** — gave unique form to Chishti sama' by introducing the **QAUL** (Arabic: **"saying"**) — a **hymn sung at the OPENING or CLOSING of qawwali**, followed by sufi poetry in **Persian, Hindavi, Urdu**; **qawwals at Nizamuddin's shrine still BEGIN with the qaul**; qawwali today performed at shrines all over the subcontinent.
- **📜 Source 7 (Dargah Quli Khan, 18th-c. Deccan visitor, MURAQQA-I DEHLI / "Album of Delhi")** on the **Nasiruddin Chiragh-i Dehli shrine**: the Shaikh *"is not the lamp of Delhi but of the ENTIRE country"*; crowds especially on **SUNDAY**; in **DIWALI month the entire population of Delhi visits**, stays in tents by the spring tank; **baths to cure chronic diseases**; **"Muslims and Hindus pay visits in the same spirit"**; merrymaking under trees.
- **📜 Source 8 — "Charkhanama" (song to the spinning-wheel rhythm):** spinning as devotion — **zikr-i jali** (taking cotton), **zikr-i qalbi** (separating), **zikr-i aini** (spooling); breath-count **24,000** offered as **gift to the PIR** — shows sufism woven into **women's household labour**.

### 7.3 Languages and communication
- Chishtis in Delhi conversed in **HINDAVI** (language of the people); **Baba Farid's verses in the local language are in the GURU GRANTH SAHIB**; long poems/**MASNAVIS** expressed divine love via human love as allegory: the **PREM-AKHYAN (love story) PADMAVAT by Malik Muhammad Jayasi** — romance of **Padmini & Ratansen, king of Chittor** = **soul's journey to the divine**; recited in hospices, often during sama'.
- **Bijapur (Karnataka) genre:** short poems in **DAKHANI** (Urdu variant) by 17th–18th c. Chishti sufis; probably **sung by WOMEN during household chores (grinding grain, spinning)**; also **LURINAMA (lullabies) & SHADINAMA (wedding songs)**; sufis inspired by **Kannada vachanas of the Lingayats & Marathi ABHANGS of the sants of PANDHARPUR**; through this medium **Islam gradually gained a place in the VILLAGES OF THE DECCAN**.

### 7.4 Sufis and the state
- **Chishti hallmark: AUSTERITY + DISTANCE from worldly power** — yet **not absolute isolation from political power**:
  - Sufis **accepted UNSOLICITED grants & donations** from political elites.
  - **Sultans set up CHARITABLE TRUSTS (AUQAF)** as endowments for hospices and granted **tax-free land (INAM)**.
  - Chishtis accepted donations **in cash & kind**, **used them fully on immediate needs** — food, clothes, living quarters, ritual necessities (sama') — **not accumulating wealth** → this **enhanced the moral authority** of shaikhs, attracting people from **all walks of life**.
  - Sufi **piety, scholarship, belief in miraculous powers** → popular with the **masses whose support kings wished to secure**.
  - Kings needed **LEGITIMATION from sufis**: Turks of the Delhi Sultanate **resisted the ulama's insistence on shari'a as state law** (feared opposition from non-Muslim majority subjects) → sought **sufis, whose authority derived DIRECTLY FROM GOD** (not from jurists interpreting shari'a).
  - It was believed **auliya could intercede with Allah** in matters outside state jurisdiction; **kings wanted their tombs near sufi dargahs** (e.g., around Nizamuddin's shrine in Delhi).

## 8. §8 NEW DEVOTIONAL PATHS: THE SAINT POETS

### 8.1 Weaving a divine fabric: KABIR (c. 14th–15th centuries)
- **Perhaps the most outstanding saint-poet**; a **weaver (julaha)** by occupation; legend makes him a **disciple of Ramananda**.
- **Critiqued the EXTERNAL practices of BOTH religions:** idolatry, pilgrimage, baths of Hindus; circumcision & rules of Muslims — ridiculed **Brahmanas & MULLAHS** alike.
- Spoke of the **formless divine: ALAKH (the unseen), NIRANJAN**; used **ULTABANSI** (paradox/riddle style — "upside-down sayings").
- Poems in **many languages & dialects** (various forms: **sakhis & pads**, **ramaini**, **shabda**) — oral transmission; compilations made **long after his death**.
- **His verses survive in 3 distinct but overlapping traditions:**
  1. **KABIJ BIJAK** — preserved by the **KABIRPANTH** (Varanasi & elsewhere in **Uttar Pradesh**).
  2. **KABIR GRANTHAVALI** — associated with the **DADUPANTH (Rajasthan)**.
  3. **Many poems in the ADI GRANTH SAHIB** (Sikh scripture).
- **Universality:** verses sung by **Muslims and Hindus alike**; **one deity, many names**.

### 8.2 Baba Guru Nanak and the Sacred Word (1469–1539)
- Born in a **HINDU MERCHANT family** in **NANKANA SAHIB village, near river RAVI** (predominantly Muslim **Punjab**); **trained as an ACCOUNTANT, studied PERSIAN**; married young; spent time among **sufis & bhaktas**; **travelled widely**.
- **Message (from his hymns/teachings):** advocated **NIRGUNA BHAKTI**; **firmly repudiated external practices of both religions — sacrifices, ritual baths, image worship, austerities, scriptures of Hindus AND Muslims**; the Absolute (**"RAB"**) had **no gender or form**.
- **Path:** **remembering & repeating the DIVINE NAME**; ideas expressed through hymns — **"SHABAD"** — in **PUNJABI**; sang in various **RAGAS** while attendant **MARDANA played the RABAB**.
- **Community:** organised followers; rules for **congregational worship (SANGAT)** — collective recitation; appointed disciple **ANGAD** as successor (**GURU**) — practice continued **nearly 200 years**.
- He **did not wish to found a new religion**, but followers **consolidated distinct practices**, separating from both Hindus & Muslims.
- **GURU ARJAN (5th preceptor):** compiled Nanak's hymns + those of his **4 successors** + other religious poets — **BABA FARID, RAVIDAS (RAIDAS), KABIR** — in the **ADI GRANTH SAHIB**; hymns = **"GURBANI"**, in various languages.
- **Late 17th century:** **GURU GOBIND SINGH (10th preceptor)** added compositions of the 9th guru, **GURU TEGH BAHADUR** → scripture called the **GURU GRANTH SAHIB**; founded the **KHALSA PANTH ("army of the pure")** with **5 symbols: uncut hair, dagger, pair of shorts, comb, steel bangle** → community consolidated as a **socio-religious AND military force**.

### 8.3 Mirabai, the devotee princess (c. 15th–16th centuries)
- **Best-known woman poet of the bhakti tradition**; biographies reconstructed from **BHAJANS attributed to her**, transmitted **orally for centuries**.
- **Rajput princess from MERTA in MARWAR**, **married against her wishes** into a family (Sisodia clan) that **rejected her devotion to KRISHNA**; defied **husband & orthodox norms**.
- **Recognised RAIDAS (Ravidas) — a leather-worker (considered "untouchable") — as her GURU** → **direct challenge to HIGH CASTE & PATRIARCHAL norms**.
- Her songs (in **Rajasthani & mixed dialects**) express **intense devotion to Krishna**; she **refused to submit** to social expectations — an inspiration for centuries of **ordinary people's devotion**; special place in **popular memory of Rajasthan & Gujarat**.

**📦 Box — SHANKARADEVA (late 15th century, Assam):** preached **BHAGAVATI DHARMA** based on the **Bhagavad Gita & Bhagavata Purana**; practices: **NAM KIRTAN** (congregational singing), **SATRA** (monastery), **NAAM GHAR** (prayer hall).
**📦 Box — Varieties of sufi texts (source-genres):**
1. **Treatises/manuals** — e.g., **KASHF-UL-MAHJUB of Ali bin Usman HUJWIRI (d. c. 1071)** — shows how traditions outside the subcontinent influenced Indian sufism.
2. **MALFUZAT** ("uttered" — conversations of saints) — **FAWA'ID-AL-FU'AD**: conversations of **Nizamuddin Auliya compiled by Amir Hasan Sijzi Dehlavi** (Persian poet); compiled with shaikhs' permission; **didactic purpose**; found across the subcontinent incl. Deccan.
3. **MAKTUBAT** ("written" — **letters by sufi masters to disciples**) — reflect the shaikh's spiritual experience + **life conditions of recipients**; e.g., **MAKTUBAT-I IMAM RABBANI of Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi (d. 1624, Naqshbandi)** — ideology **contrasted with Akbar's liberal, non-sectarian views**.
4. **TAZKIRAS** ("to mention & memorialise" — **biographies of saints**) — **SIYAR-UL-AULIYA by Mir Khwurd Kirmani** (14th c. — **first sufi tazkira written in India**, on Chishti saints); most famous: **AKHBAR-UL-AKHYAR by Abdul Haqq Muhaddis Dehlavi** (covers 16th c.).
**📜 Timeline — Major religious teachers (approx. periods):**
- c. 500–800: **Appar, Sambandar, Sundaramurti** (Tamil Nadu) • c. 800–900: **Nammalvar, Manikkavachakar, Andal, Tondaradippodi** (TN) • c. 1000–1100: **Al Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh, Punjab); Ramanujacharya** (TN) • c. 1100–1200: **Basavanna** (Karnataka) • c. 1200–1300: **Jnanadeva, Muktabai** (Maharashtra); **Muinuddin Chishti** (Rajasthan); **Bahauddin Zakariyya, Fariduddin Ganj-i Shakar** (Punjab); **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** (Delhi) • c. 1300–1400: **Lal Ded** (Kashmir); **Lal Shahbaz Qalandar** (Sind); **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Delhi); **Ramananda** (UP); **Chokhamela** (Maharashtra); **Sharafuddin Yahya Maneri** (Bihar) • c. 1400–1500: **Kabir, Raidas, Surdas** (UP); **Baba Guru Nanak** (Punjab); **Vallabhacharya** (Gujarat); **Abdullah Shattari** (Gwalior); **Muhammad Shah Alam** (Gujarat); **Mir Sayyid Muhammad Gesu Daraz** (Gulbarga); **Shankaradeva** (Assam); **Tukaram** (Maharashtra) • c. 1500–1600: **Sri Chaitanya** (Bengal); **Mirabai** (Rajasthan); **Shaikh Abdul Quddus Gangohi, Malik Muhammad Jaisi, Tulsidas** (UP) • c. 1600–1700: **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** (Haryana); **Miyan Mir** (Punjab).
- **Conclusion of chapter (historian's note):** sources range from **simple direct vachanas of Basavanna** to **ornate Persian farmans** of Mughal emperors — each genre needs **different skills (languages + style awareness)**; religious traditions are **dynamic, changing over time**.

## 1. §1 THE DISCOVERY OF HAMPI
- **COLIN MACKENZIE** (born **1754**) — engineer, surveyor, antiquarian; became the **FIRST SURVEYOR GENERAL OF INDIA** (d. 1821).
- **1800:** produced the **FIRST SURVEY MAP of the HAMPI RUINS** (site of the ancient capital); gathered information from **local informants + oral traditions** ("ancient accounts"); believed that *"the minute and critical study of Indian antiquities... of art and literature would be better calculated to preserve the memory of the people... than any other way."*
- **Antiquarian study sequence:** Mackenzie's map → (mid-19th c.) photographers → **1856: Alexander Greenlaw** took the **first photographs** (~45 calotypes of the site) → epigraphists (inscription experts) → archaeologists → a detailed **World Heritage** reconstruction of the city.
- **🖼️ Fig 7.2:** Mackenzie with assistants (painted by an Indian artist, c. 1815) — collecting information on the ground.
- **Sources for Vijayanagara's history (box):** (i) **Inscriptions** of Vijayanagara kings & their **nayakas** — recording **donations to temples & important events**; (ii) **Travellers' accounts**:

## 2. §2 RAYAS, NAYAKAS: KINGS, TRADERS AND THE EMPIRE

### 2.1 Kings and traders
- Warfare depended on **effective CAVALRY** → **import of HORSES from Arabia & Central Asia** was crucial; trade initially **controlled by ARAB TRADERS**.
- Local **horse merchants: KUDIRAI CHETTIS** participated in the trade.
- **From 1498:** the **PORTUGUESE** arrived on the **west coast**, establishing **trading & military stations**; their **superior military technology (MUSKETS)** made them key players in the period's tangled politics.
- Vijayanagara markets: **spices, textiles, PRECIOUS STONES**; trade = **status symbol**; a **wealthy population demanded high-value exotic goods** (precious stones, jewellery); **revenue from trade strengthened the empire**.
- **📦 Box — "Elephants, horses and men":** **GAJAPATI** = "lord of **elephants**" (ruling lineage of **Orissa**, 15th c.); in Vijayanagara popular tradition, the **Deccan Sultans = ASHVAPATI** ("lord of **horses**"); the **RAYAS = NARAPATI** ("lord of **men**").

### 2.2 The apogee and decline of the empire
- **Four dynasties:** **SANGAMA** (founder-brothers **HARIHARA I & BUKKA I**) → ruled till **1485** → **SALUVA** (military chiefs) → **TULUVA** → **ARAVIDU**.
- **KRISHNADEVA RAYA (ruled 1509–29)** — **most famous ruler**, of the **TULUVA dynasty**:
  - Characterised by **expansion & consolidation** — **defeated the GAJAPATI rulers of Orissa**; **subordinated the Deccan sultans** (one of the most **erudite men of letters** of his age).
  - **Building record:** some of the **finest temples** of the empire, incl. **impressive GOPURAMS** and **halls; eastern gopuram of Virupaksha temple; hall in front of the main shrine to mark his ACCESSION**.
  - **Literary work: AMUKTAMALYADA** (on **Telugu** — dedicated? composed on Andal's hymns; royal ideal text).
  - Support to temples: e.g., support of Kannada/Tamil Vaishnava traditions; capital at its **peak of prosperity**.
- **After his death (1529):** struggle among successors; **1552? — power shifted to the ARAVIDU dynasty** (ruled from **PENUKONDA**, eastern focus).
- **1565 — BATTLE OF RAKSHASA-TANGADI (TALIKOTA):** **Rama Raya** (de facto ruler) defeated by an **alliance of Deccan sultans**; Rama Raya **executed**; the capital **SACKED, plundered & ABANDONED** — its ruins remain.
- **📜 Source 1 — "The fate of Rama Raya" (from Paes/Nuniz accounts & later traditions):** Rama Raya, though aged, stood with his troops; sultans' treachery/defeat — he was **seized & beheaded on the battlefield**; his head shown to the army — the army scattered; the city fell. (Nuance: sources vary in details.)

### 2.3 The rayas and the nayakas
- **NAYAKAS:** **military chiefs** — usually **Telugu or Kannada speakers**; controlled **forts & armed supporters**; came to power either by **conquering areas** or via **royal appointment**; many were **rebellious but not beyond control** — kings could **transfer/replace** them.
- **AMARA-NAYAKA SYSTEM** = major political innovation; **AMARA** believed derived from Sanskrit (scholars debate — from "commander of a thousand"? or related to revenue term).
- **Duties of amara-nayakas (4):**
  1. **Collected TAXES & other dues** from peasants, traders, artisans of the **amara** (assignment area).
  2. **Retained PART of the revenue** for **personal use + maintaining TROOPS, HORSES, elephants**.
  3. **Sent ANNUAL TRIBUTE** to the king.
  4. **Furnished MILITARY CONTINGENTS** to the king when required.
- System resembles the **IQTA system of the Delhi Sultanate** (scholars' comparison).
- **Post-1565:** powerful nayakas **established INDEPENDENT KINGDOMS** — **MYSORE, KELADI (IKKERI), MADURAI, TANJAVUR**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Dynasties — "**S-S-T-A**": Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva, Aravidu ("Students Should Take Action"). Amara-nayaka duties — "**T-R-T-T**": Taxes, Retain, Tribute, Troops. Krishnadeva Raya — "**1509-29, Amuktamalyada, Gajapati+Sultans defeated**".

## 3. §3 VIJAYANAGARA: THE CAPITAL AND ITS ENVIRONS
- City surrounded by **seven lines of FORTS**; the **fortified zone enclosed NOT ONLY the urban core BUT ALSO AGRICULTURAL TRACTS & IRRIGATED VALLEYS** — defence + self-sufficiency.
- **Walls built of STONE & RUBBLE (with mortar)**; **GATEWAYS with ARCHED openings** (arch influence: Indo-Islamic techniques); watch-towers; gateways often **built/donated by kings & nayakas** (inscriptions).
- **ROADS:** the city was served by roads radiating from the **royal centre**; gateways regulated entry (tolls); roads linked the **royal centre ↔ sacred centre ↔ suburbs**.
- **Zones of the city:** the **ROYAL CENTRE** and the **SACRED CENTRE** (distinct areas) + suburban agricultural settlements.

### 3.1 Water resources
- Natural basin **fed by the TUNGABHADRA river** + **canals** drawing water:
  - **HIRIYA CANAL** — among the **most prominent**, drew water **from a DAM across the Tungabhadra**.
  - **KAMALAPURAM TANK** (15th century) — **irrigation + water to the ROYAL CENTRE**.
- **AQUEDUCTS** carried water **into the ROYAL CENTRE** (e.g., to the **royal tank**? — the **stepped stone tank** in the sacred centre is fed by aqueducts).
- **Stepped stone tank (sacred centre)** — an elegant water structure.

### 3.2 The urban core
- The **ROYAL CENTRE**: house of the king & his household + **temples (over 60?) — many temples & shrines**; **"KING'S PALACE" = the LARGEST complex** (identified by **Domingo Paes**); palace buildings **often of wood & clay brick? — more fragile materials**, hence poorly preserved vs stone temples.

## 4. §4 THE ROYAL CENTRE

### 4.1 The Mahanavami dibba
- **MAHANAVAMI DIBBA** = massive **PLATFORM** on **one of the highest points** of the city: **base ~11,000 sq. ft; height 40 FT**; evidence of a **WOODEN SUPERSTRUCTURE**; base covered with **relief carvings**.
- **Rituals: MAHANAVAMI** ("the great ninth day") of the **TEN-DAY HINDU FESTIVAL** during **autumn (September–October)** — known as **DUSEHRA (northern India), DURGA PUJA (Bengal), NAVARATRI/MAHANAVAMI (peninsular India)**.
- **The ceremony (from Paes):** the king **inspected the army & the fleet? — army and its horses**, received **tribute & gifts from nayakas**, watched **dances, wrestling matches, processions of horses/elephants? — grand processions** — display of **WEALTH & POWER**; feast of guests.
- **📜 Source 2 — Paes: "A House of Victory"** (audience hall + dibba together called the "House of Victory"): *"These buildings have TWO PLATFORMS ONE ABOVE THE OTHER, beautifully sculpted… On the upper platform… the king has a room made of cloth… where the IDOL has a shrine… and in the other in the middle is placed a DAIS on which stands a THRONE OF STATE (the CROWN and the ROYAL ANKLET)…"* — the king **sat daily in durbar** during the festival.
- **Audience hall:** high platform with **wooden pillars? — a hall supported by pillars** at the top (wood decayed); **no free floor space** → use uncertain; possibly **the hall where the king received guests**.

### 4.2 Other buildings in the royal centre
- **LOTUS MAHAL** — *"one of the most beautiful buildings"*; name given by **19th-century BRITISH travellers**; function unknown — a **map by MACKENZIE suggests it was a COUNCIL CHAMBER** (where the king met advisers); features **arches of INDO-ISLAMIC style**; **9 towers — 1 high central + 8 along the sides** (elevation drawing shows them).
- **"ELEPHANT STABLES"** — building close to the Lotus Mahal; historians question the identification.
- **HAZARA RAMA TEMPLE** — *"one of the most spectacular"* royal-centre temples; **probably used ONLY BY THE KING & HIS FAMILY**; central-shrine **images missing**, but **sculpted panels survive** — **scenes from the RAMAYANA** on the inner walls; also **elephants, horses, dancing women** carved.
- **Nayaka continuation:** after the sack, **nayakas CONTINUED the traditions of building palatial structures** (e.g., **audience hall at MADURAI** — with arches) — showing the **architectural legacy of Vijayanagara**.

## 5. §5 THE SACRED CENTRE

### 5.1 Choosing a capital
- **Rocky northern end, banks of the TUNGABHADRA.**
- **Local tradition:** these hills = **MONKEY KINGDOM of VALI & SUGRIVA** (the **RAMAYANA**).
- **Other traditions:** **PAMPADEVI** (local mother goddess) **did penance in these hills to MARRY VIRUPAKSHA** — **guardian deity of the kingdom, a FORM OF SHIVA**; hills named after Pampa; hence the goddess **Pampa** sanctified the site; the capital = **"city of victory"** (Vijayanagara).
- **Title note:** the raya took titles linking to deities — e.g., Krishnadeva Raya's inscriptions associate with Virupaksha.

### 5.2 Temples, gopurams and mandapas
- **Temple-building = royal/nayaka patronage; inscriptions record donations** — temples show **elaborate royal ritual connections** (king = protector of dharma).
- **Distinctive architectural features (2 signature terms):**
  - **GOPURAM** = **tall ROYAL GATEWAY** to the temple complex.
  - **MANDAPA** = **pavilion**; **long pillared corridors** ran around shrines; **KALYANA MANDAPA** = pavilion to **celebrate DIVINE WEDDINGS**.
- **VIRUPAKSHA TEMPLE:**
  - Built **over centuries** — **earliest shrine dated 9th–10th centuries** (inscriptions); **substantially enlarged with the Vijayanagara Empire**.
  - **Hall in front of the main shrine built by KRISHNADEVA RAYA TO MARK HIS ACCESSION** — **delicately carved pillars**; he also credited with the **EASTERN GOPURAM**.
  - Additions meant the **central shrine became a relatively SMALL part** of the complex.
  - **Temple halls' uses:** (i) **images of gods placed to WITNESS special programmes of music, dance, drama**; (ii) **celebrating MARRIAGES of deities (kalyana mandapa)**; (iii) **SWING pavilions** — deities swung on swings; **special images** (distinct from the small central-shrine idol) used on these occasions.
- **VITTHALA TEMPLE:**
  - Principal deity: **VITTHALA — a form of VISHNU generally worshipped in MAHARASHTRA**.
  - **Introduction of Vitthala worship into Karnataka = example of how Vijayanagara rulers DREW ON DIFFERENT TRADITIONS to create an IMPERIAL CULTURE.**
  - **Several halls + a UNIQUE SHRINE DESIGNED AS A CHARIOT** (stone chariot).
  - **CHARIOT STREETS:** extended **from the temple GOPURAM in a STRAIGHT LINE**; **paved with stone slabs**, lined with **pillared pavilions where MERCHANTS set up shops** — temple = commercial hub too.
- **Nayaka legacy:** nayakas continued & elaborated **temple-building traditions**; **some of the most spectacular GOPURAMS were built by the NAYAKAS** (e.g., at Madurai, Thanjavur region).

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Sacred-centre pair — "**V&V**": **Virupaksha** (Shiva, 9th–10th c. origin, Krishnadeva's accession hall + eastern gopuram) & **Vitthala** (Vishnu-from-Maharashtra, chariot shrine, chariot streets). Signature terms — "**G-M-K**": Gopuram, Mandapa, Kalyana mandapa.

## 1. §1 PEASANTS AND AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION

### 1.1 Looking for sources
- **Primary source: AIN-I AKBARI ("the Ain") — authored by ABU'L FAZL**, Akbar's court historian (part of the **Akbar Nama**).
- Chronicle aim = **court's vision** — hence historians **supplement** it with: **local revenue records, deshwala? — village documents, Persian chronicles, EUROPEAN TRAVELLER accounts** (Ralph Fitch? — the chapter cites travellers like **Pelsaert, Tavernier** in boxes), **East India Company records** & regional language sources.
- Both **peasants AND landed elites** were involved in agricultural production and **claimed rights to a share of the produce**.

### 1.2 Peasants and their lands
- **Terms for peasant (Indo-Persian): RAIYAT** (pl. **RIAYA**) or **MUZARIAN**; also **KISAN** and **ASAMI**.
- **Two kinds of peasants (17th-c. sources):**
  - **KHUD-KASHTA** = **residents of the village in which they held their lands**.
  - **PAHI-KASHTA** = **NON-RESIDENT cultivators** who belonged to **another village** but **cultivated lands elsewhere on a CONTRACTUAL basis**.
  - **Became pahi-kashta out of:** (i) **CHOICE** — e.g., **more favourable revenue terms** in a distant village; (ii) **COMPULSION** — e.g., **economic distress after a FAMINE**.
- **Average north-Indian peasant** seldom owned **more than a few acres**; **monsoon dependence**; two harvesting seasons: **KHARIF** (autumn — rice, etc.) and **RABI** (spring — wheat, etc.).

### 1.3 Irrigation and technology
- **Basic technology:** wooden ploughshare, hoe, **WELLS** — remained **normal through the ages**.
- **Well-irrigation devices:** leather **CHANGI? — CHARAS** (leather bag lifted by a pair of oxen); **DHENKLI** (lever-based lift).
- **Irrigation projects were built by the STATE as well as by PRIVATE individuals** — **zamindars & peasants** invested in wells/tanks (not only state efforts) — shows **private agrarian enterprise**.
- **Monsoon-dependent agriculture** — irrigation **reduced risk**, raised yields.

### 1.4 An abundance of crops
- **Two harvests/year = DO-FASLA** in most regions (except most arid); some irrigated areas even **three crops**.
- **Crop varieties (from the Ain):** **AGRA — 39 varieties** (two seasons); **DELHI — 43 varieties**; **BENGAL — 50 varieties of RICE alone**.
- **JINS-I KAMIL** (literally "**perfect crops**"): crops **encouraged by the Mughal state** as they **brought higher revenue** — **COTTON & SUGARCANE = jins-i kamil PAR EXCELLENCE**; cotton grown across **central India & the Deccan plateau**; **Bengal famous for SUGAR**; also **oilseeds (e.g., MUSTARD)**.
- Agriculture was **NOT only subsistence** — **commercial/cash-crop cultivation** linked villages to markets.

Numbers — "**39-43-50**" (Agra-Delhi-Bengal/rice). Jins-i-kamil — "**C-S-O**" (Cotton, Sugarcane, Oilseeds/mustard).

## 2. §2 THE VILLAGE COMMUNITY

### 2.1 Caste and the rural milieu
- Cultivators = **highly HETEROGENEOUS group**; deep **caste-based inequities**.
- Sizeable number worked as **MENIALS/agricultural labourers (MAJUR)**.
- Despite **abundant cultivable land**, certain **caste groups were ASSIGNED MENIAL TASKS** → **relegated to poverty**.
- Such groups = **large section of village population**, **least resources**, **constrained by caste hierarchy** — *"much like the DALITS of modern India."*
- **📦 Box — "Money in the village" (Tavernier, 17th c.):** every non-tiny village had a **money-changer = SHROFF** — acted as **bankers, made remittances**, **exchanged rupee ↔ paisa ↔ cowrie shells** at their own rates.

### 2.2 Panchayats and headmen
- **PANCHAYAT** = assembly of **ELDERS**, usually people with **HEREDITARY rights over property**; in **mixed-caste villages** = **heterogeneous** body; an **OLIGARCHY** (not democracy); **village menial-cum-agricultural workers UNLIKELY to be represented**; **decisions BINDING on members**.
- **Headman = MUQADDAM or MANDAL:** chosen by **consensus of village elders**; choice **RATIFIED BY THE ZAMINDAR**; held office while enjoying **elders' confidence** — else **dismissed by them**; **chief function: SUPERVISE PREPARATION OF VILLAGE ACCOUNTS**, assisted by the **accountant = PATWARI** (also kept panchayat records; maintained records at the **PARGANA** level for the state too).
- **Panchayat FUNDS:** **1% levy added to the land revenue** (community fund) — used for **famine relief? — welfare: helping the poor, festivals, repairs** (community expenses).
- **Functions:** **settled disputes, enforced contracts, ensured fair conduct**; **regulated MORAL CONDUCT** — e.g., fined/expelled those who broke norms; the most feared punishment = **EXPULSION from the community (out-casting)**.
- **Women & the panchayat:** documents from **Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra** record **PETITIONS BY WOMEN** to panchayats — protesting **husbands' infidelity or neglect** by the **GRIHASTHI** (male head of household); male infidelity **not always punished**, but **state & "superior" castes intervened to ensure the family was provided for**; in records, women's **names were EXCLUDED** — petitioner listed as **mother/sister/wife of the male head**.

### 2.3 Village artisans
- **Elaborate relationship of EXCHANGE between producers**: **Marathi documents & early-British village surveys** — artisans sometimes **25% of village HOUSEHOLDS**.
- Distinction between **artisan & peasant sometimes BLURRED** (artisans also cultivated).
- Systems of service exchange: **BALUTEDAR system (Maharashtra — 18–20 artisan families serving the village)**; in north India the analogous **JAJMANI system** — artisan families served peasants/gentry, receiving **grain/seasonal payments**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Panchayat chain — "**E-O-M-P-1%**": Elders' Oligarchy → Muqaddam → Patwari → 1% fund. Headman check — "**C-R-C**": Consensus, Ratified (by zamindar), Confidence (removable). Artisans — "**25% / B&J**" (25% households; Balutedar & Jajmani).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** 8-marker goldmine: "Describe the village panchayat & its functions." Include: composition (hereditary elders, oligarchy, menials excluded), muqaddam selection/removal, patwari, 1% fund, moral regulation/expulsion, women's petitions (name exclusion). MCQs: shroff = money-changer; balutedar = Maharashtra.

## 3. §3 WOMEN IN AGRARIAN SOCIETY
- Men & women worked **"shoulder to shoulder"** — gendered division: **MEN TILLED & PLOUGHED; WOMEN SOWED, WEEDING, THRESHING, WINNOWING** the harvest.
- **Women's ARTISANAL tasks:** **spinning yarn, sifting & kneading CLAY for pottery, EMBROIDERY**; paddy **transplantation**; **entering BETEL-LEAF (paan) GROVES in BENGAL** (women's presence needed for pollination? — entry reserved to women).
- **"The more COMMERCIALISED the product, the greater the demand on WOMEN'S LABOUR"** — women worked **in fields, at employers' houses, and in MARKETS**.
- Women = **important resource as CHILD-BEARERS** in a **labour-dependent society**; **HIGH MORTALITY** among women (**malnutrition, frequent pregnancies, deaths in childbirth**) → **shortage of wives** → distinct social customs of peasant/artisan communities:
  - **BRIDE-PRICE** (not dowry) paid **to the bride's family** in many rural communities.
  - **REMARRIAGE legitimate for DIVORCED & WIDOWED women.**
- **Control & patriarchy:** household **headed by a MALE**; women kept under **strict control**; **draconian punishments** suspected on **infidelity**.
- **Property rights of women (landed gentry):** women had the **right to INHERIT PROPERTY**; instances from the **PUNJAB** — women incl. **widows** **actively sold inherited property in the rural land market**; **Hindu AND Muslim women inherited ZAMINDARIS** — **free to sell or mortgage**; **WOMEN ZAMINDARS known in 18th-century BENGAL** (one of the biggest & most famous was a woman).

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Gender map — "**Field: S-W-T-W; Craft: S-C-E**" (Sowed-Weeded-Threshed-Winnowed; Spinning-Clay-Embroidery). Marriage flip — "**P-R**": bride-Price + Remarriage (peasants) vs dowry + no-remarriage (elites).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** Favourite 3-marker: "Women's roles & rights in agrarian society" — include BOTH labour roles AND property/inheritance rights (women zamindars of Bengal) — most students miss the rights half.

## 4. §4 FORESTS AND TRIBES

### 4.1 Beyond settled villages
- Apart from intensively cultivated north/north-west: **huge forests — JANGAL (dense forest) or KHARBANDI (scrubland)** — across **eastern, central, northern India (incl. the TERAI on the Indo-Nepal border), JHARKHAND, the Western Ghats & the Deccan plateau**.
- **Estimated forest cover: ~40% average** (informed conjecture; no census then).
- Forest dwellers termed **"JANGLI"** in contemporary texts — did **NOT mean absence of "civilisation"**; it described livelihood from **gathering forest produce, HUNTING & SHIFTING AGRICULTURE** — **season-specific**:
  - **BHLIS' calendar:** SPRING = collecting forest produce; SUMMER = FISHING; MONSOON = CULTIVATION; AUTUMN+WINTER = HUNTING.
  - Such sequences **presumed & perpetuated MOBILITY** — distinctive feature of forest tribes.
- **For the STATE, the forest =** (i) **place of REFUGE (mlechchhas, outlaws — daku? runaways)** and (ii) **source of ELEPHANTS** for the army (demanded as tribute/peshkash).

### 4.2 Inroads into forests
- **Agricultural expansion pressed INTO forests**: individuals/groups **cleared land & settled**, attracting **peasant immigrants**; **state encouraged** this (more revenue).
- **Tribal chiefs became ZAMINDARS**, some **joined imperial service**; tribalsoldiers recruited; **intense interaction** between tribal & settled societies — tribes traded **forest produce** (honey, gums, timber?) for grain/cloth/salt.
- **Examples of tribes:** **KHOKHAR, BHIL, GOND, SANTHAL, KOL, AHER? — KOLIS; BANJARAS** = itinerant trader-grain carriers (ox-caravans, long-distance grain transport for armies/markets).

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Bhil seasons — "**S-S-M-A**": Spring-Stuff, Summer-Swim (fishing), Monsoon-Mow (cultivation), Autumn/Winter-Aim (hunting). Forest duo — "**R-E**": Refuge & Elephants.
**🎯 Exam Focus:** 3-marker: "jangli did not mean uncivilised — explain" (livelihood-based seasonality + mobility). MCQ: 40% forest-cover conjecture; Banjaras = traders.

## 5. §5 THE ZAMINDARS
- **ZAMINDARS** = people who **lived OFF agriculture WITHOUT DIRECTLY participating** in production — **landed proprietors** with **social-economic privileges by virtue of superior status**.
- **Two pillars of status:** (i) **CASTE** (high castes — most zamindars were **RAJPUTS**; also **JATS** etc.); (ii) **KHIDMAT** — **services to the STATE** (e.g., troopers).
- **MILKIYAT** = **extensive PERSONAL lands** of the zamindar — cultivated for **private use, often with HIRED or SERVILE labour**; zamindars could **SELL, BEQUEATH or MORTGAGE milkiyat at will**.
- **Powers & functions:**
  - **Collected revenue ON BEHALF OF THE STATE** (zamindari rights often = revenue-collection rights).
  - Levied **EXTRA TAXES/cesses** on **markets, festivals, passing traders**.
  - **SOLD/BOUGHT zamindaris**; zamindari could be acquired by **(i) lineage/ancestry (hereditary clan right — e.g., Rajput clans), (ii) PURCHASE, (iii) STATE GRANTS/REWARDS (incl. madad-i-maash to service families), (iv) SETTLING new villages (founding rights)**.
  - Maintained **FORTS (mud fortresses), armed men, CAVALY**; gave **CREDIT (loans)** to peasants; controlled **minor markets**.
- **Continuity:** hereditary rights made zamindars the **MOST STABLE element** in rural society (dynasties of peasants rose/fell; zamindari houses persisted).
- **Extra fact:** **Rajputs & Jats** adopted zamindari strategies; **caste-zamindari correlation strong**; zamindars also **shared the produce** of tenants.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Power pillars — "**C-K-M-R**": Caste, Khidmat, Milkiyat, Revenue-collection. Acquire 4 ways — "**B-P-G-S**": Birth, Purchase, Grant, Settlement.
**🎯 Exam Focus:** 8-marker: "Role & powers of zamindars" — definition + pillars + milkiyat + revenue collection + military + credit + stability. Link to revolts: squeezed zamindars led peasant uprisings (Jats, Satnamis, Sikhs, Marathas).

## 6. §6 LAND REVENUE SYSTEM
- **Revenue from land = ECONOMIC MAINSTAY** of the Mughal Empire → elaborate **administrative apparatus**.
- **Office of the DIWAN (DAFTAR)** supervised the **fiscal system** of the empire; **revenue officials & record-keepers PENETRATED the agricultural domain** — became a **DECISIVE AGENT in shaping agrarian relations**.
- **Two stages:** (1) **ASSESSMENT** (2) **COLLECTION**.
  - **JAMA** = amount **ASSESSED**; **HASIL** = amount **COLLECTED** (jama often ≠ hasil → inflated assessments).
- **Akbar's decree to the AMIL-GUZAR (revenue collector):** strive to make cultivators **pay in CASH**, but **option of payment in KIND to be kept open**.
- State tried to **MAXIMISE claims**; actual realisation **thwarted by local conditions**.
- **Measurement:** BOTH **cultivated & cultivable** lands measured in each province; the **Ain compiled aggregates**; measurement continued later — e.g., **1665: Aurangzeb expressly ordered ANNUAL RECORDS of cultivators in each village**; **forests remained UNMEASURED** (huge areas).
- **Pargana officials:** **SHIQDAR** (executive/police), **QANUNGO** (record-keeper of revenue documents), **PATWARI** (village accountant), **AMIN** (assessment/market oversight — ensured **imperial regulations carried out** in provinces), plus **FOTADAR** (treasurer).
- **Abu'l Fazl's methods of ASSESSMENT (4):**
  1. **ZABTI** — based on **MEASUREMENT** of land + average prices → **MOST WIDESPREAD**.
  2. **BATAI / GHALLA-BHAKSHI** — **division of PRODUCE** between cultivator & state — **most TRANSPARENT** (protection in bad harvests).
  3. **KANKUT** — **ESTIMATION** of the standing crop (grain-heap estimation).
  4. **NASAQ** — **summary/rough appraisal** based on **past assessments**.
- **ZABTI / DAHSALA system (from 1580):** evolved by **RAJA TODAR MAL** — 10-year average of **produce AND prices** → standard schedules (**DASTUR**) for each province.
- **📜 Source 5 — "Classification of lands under Akbar" (Ain):** *"Polaj = land annually cultivated for each crop in succession, NEVER allowed to lie FALLOW. Parauti = land LEFT OUT of cultivation for a time that it may RECOVER ITS STRENGTH. Chachar = land fallow THREE OR FOUR YEARS. Banjar = land uncultivated FIVE YEARS AND MORE. Of the first two kinds there are THREE classes — GOOD, MIDDLING, BAD. They add together the produce of each sort, and the THIRD of this represents the MEDIUM produce, ONE-THIRD PART of which is exacted as the ROYAL DUES."*
- ** implication:** state's share = **1/3 of the average produce** (in cash under zabti); fairness via **averaging good/middling/bad**.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Jama-Hasil — "**A-C**" pair. Methods — "**Z-B-K-N**" = "**Zebras Bathe Kindly Nightly**" (Zabti, Batai, Kankut, Nasaq). Land classes — "**P-P-C-B**": **0 / temporary / 3-4 yrs / 5+ yrs** fallow ("People Prefer Coffee Breaks"). Officials — "**SQPA**": Shiqdar, Qanungo, Patwari, Amin. Numbers — "**1580 Todar Mal; 1/3 share; 10-yr dastur**".
**🎯 Exam Focus:** The most-tested zone of Theme 8: source-based on the classification extract (Qs: principles of classification — fertility + continuity; averaging; limitation of Ain data) + 8-marker "describe the zabti/dahsala system". MCQs: jama vs hasil; amil-guzar; 1665 Aurangzeb survey; 1/3 royal dues.

## 7. §7 THE FLOW OF SILVER
- 16th–17th centuries: **four large territorial empires of Asia consolidated power & resources:** **MUGHAL (India), MING (China), SAFAVID (Iran), OTTOMAN (Turkey)**.
- Their **political stability** created **vibrant overland trade networks from CHINA to the MEDITERRANEAN**.
- **Voyages of discovery + opening of the NEW WORLD** → **massive expansion of India's trade with Europe** → greater **geographical diversity & commodity composition** of India's overseas trade.
- **Expanding trade brought HUGE SILVER BULLION into Asia** to pay for Indian goods; a **large part gravitated to INDIA** — good for India as it **had NO natural resources of SILVER**.
- Result (16th–18th c.): **remarkable STABILITY of metal currency**, especially the **SILVER RUPYA** (issued by Akbar — Fig; also by Aurangzeb) → **unprecedented expansion of MINTING & money circulation** + the state's ability to **extract taxes in CASH**.
- **📜 Source 8 — Italian traveller GIOVANNI CARERI (passed through India c. 1690):** gives a **graphic account of how SILVER TRAVELLED ACROSS THE GLOBE to India** — evidences **phenomenal cash & commodity transactions** of 17th-century India.

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Empires quartet — "**M-M-S-O**": Mughal, Ming, Safavid, Ottoman. Silver chain — "**New World → Europe → India**" (India had NO silver mines → currency stability → cash revenue).
**🎯 Exam Focus:** 3-marker: "Why did silver flow into India & why does it matter?" → trade surplus + no native silver + rupya + monetisation of revenue. MCQ: Giovanni Careri c. 1690.

## 8. §8 THE AIN-I AKBARI OF ABU'L FAZL ALLAMI
- **Author: ABU'L FAZL** (Akbar's court historian, friend & ideologue); the **Ain = third book? part of the Akbar Nama**; completed in the **1590s** (revised till c. 1598?).
- **Purpose:** to present **the VISION OF THE EMPIRE** — a **comprehensive vision of SOCIAL HARMONY** in which every class & creed had a place — *"a check against the capricious whims? — guidance for officials"*; whatever contradicted that vision was seen as **"predestined to fail."**
- **Structure — FIVE BOOKS (DAFTARS); first three = administration:**
  1. **Book 1 — MANZIL-ABADI:** the **IMPERIAL HOUSEHOLD** & its maintenance.
  2. **Book 2 — SIPAH-ABADI:** **military & civil administration**, establishment of servants; **notices & short biographical sketches of MANSABDARS, learned men, poets, artists**.
  3. **Book 3 — MULK-ABADI:** the **FISCAL side** — rich **quantitative information on REVENUE RATES** + the **"ACCOUNT OF THE TWELVE PROVINCES (SUBAS)"**: geographic, topographic & economic profiles; **administrative-fiscal divisions (SARKARS, PARGANAS, MAHALS)**; **total measured area (ARAZI / ZAMIN-I PAIMUDA)**; **assessed revenue (JAMA)**.
     - **Tables per sarkar — 8 columns:** (1) pargana/mahal; (2) **QILA** (forts); (3) arazi & zamin-i paimuda (measured area); (4) **NAQDI** (revenue in cash); (5) **SUYURGHAL** (grants of revenue in charity); (6) **ZAMINDARS**; (7–8) **CASTES of zamindars + their TROOPS** — horsemen (**SAWAR**), foot-soldiers (**PIYADA**), elephants (**FIL**).
  4. **Books 4 & 5:** religious, literary & **chronometric? — cultural** matters — the **religious & literary imperial establishment**, grandees of the empire → help historians **reconstruct the social fabric** of the time.
- **Value:** *"a MINE OF INFORMATION"* on the Mughal Empire under Akbar — BUT: **"a view of the regions FROM THE CENTRE, a view of society FROM ITS APEX."**
- **Cautions (problems in using the Ain):**
  - **Numerical data often ambiguous/unreliable**; totals & conversions unclear.
  - **Provinces were NOT homogeneous** — regional variation flattened.
  - Data **abstracted from real contexts** (e.g., crops listed without acreage contexts).
  - **Idealised court-centred view** (omissions — e.g., **tobacco & maize** absent).
- **Historians' use:** e.g., **IRFAN HABIB — *THE AGRARIAN SYSTEM OF MUGHAL INDIA*** built on the Ain to reconstruct agrarian relations (wages, prices, revenue, rural stratification).

**🧠 Memory Hack:** Daftars — "**H-A-F**": Household (manzil), Army/Admin (sipah), Fiscal (mulk) + 2 cultural books. Mulk-abadi keywords — "**12 subas; 8 columns; S-P-M**" (sarkar-pargana-mahal). Critique — "**N-R-O-V**": Numbers, Regions, Omissions, View-from-apex.
**🎯 Exam Focus:** Guaranteed source zone: "Classification of lands" (already in §6). 8-marker: "The Ain as a source — value AND limitations" — must give BOTH sides + Irfan Habib. Definitions: jama, hasil, naqdi, suyurghal, sawar/piyada/fil.
